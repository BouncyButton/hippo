What the constraints are doing

  • nnUNet: bands provide +7.55 pp. Bands+SDF+depth reaches +10.15 pp. Adding edge to bands reduces Dice in every pair (−1.09 pp on average). SDF alone reduces Dice by 2.04 pp.
  • SwinUNETR: depth is the strongest single tested addition (+8.24 pp); bands add +3.85 pp. The full combination reaches +11.97 pp, although much of that gain comes from the weak seed-0 baseline.
  • UNETR: bands help in all nine pairs (+3.89 pp). Bands+edge+SDF reaches +5.35 pp. Adding depth to that combination lowers the mean by 0.47 pp, so more constraints are not automatically better.
  • 3D U-Net: bands add +4.83 pp and bands+edge adds +5.71 pp. Bands+edge+depth reaches +6.17 pp. The full combination is essentially tied at −0.10 pp relative to that best mean.

nnUNet’s improvement primarily reduces missed foreground (−33.5% at its best mean), with FP increasing 4.5%. SwinUNETR, UNETR and 3D U-Net chiefly reduce excess foreground predictions (FP −59.9%, −23.8%, −42.2%), at the cost of increased FN. Dice improves because this tradeoff is favorable overall. The recovery report for the 324-run continuation additionally records at least 0.2073 PFLOPs of optimization overhead and 0.0036 PFLOPs of calibration overhead. Seven old truncated ledgers prevent exact recovery-cost totals. There are zero unfinished compute charges. A completed trajectory’s cap does not include recovery/replay or calibration.

nnUNet uses native foreground Dice+CE, deep supervision and SGD with 60% auxiliary-gradient targets and a 75% p95 ceiling. The other three use MONAI Dice including background and AdamW, with 10% targets and a 50% p95 ceiling. Architecture, baseline loss, optimizer, and constraint strength are therefore confounded: the within-model comparisons are stronger evidence than a claim that one architecture is intrinsically superior.
