\section*{Abstract}

Segmentation annotations describe both voxel classes and the geometry of
the labelled regions. This thesis investigates whether making that spatial
information explicit during learning improves three-dimensional
segmentation when labelled examples and training computation are limited.
Four forms of annotation-derived supervision are expressed in the
LTN/logLTN framework and evaluated individually and in combination.
The investigation connects hippocampus segmentation in MRI, using MSD
and ADNI, with salt-body segmentation in synthetic GeoFWI3D volumes.

Two learning settings are examined: training volumetric models from
scratch under specified computational budgets, and adapting pretrained
segmentation models with few labelled volumes and short update schedules.
Comparisons use three folds and three seeds. Across the four architectures
trained from scratch, the highest-mean configurations improve Dice over
their matched baselines by 5.35--11.97 percentage points on MSD,
1.03--5.54 points on ADNI and 1.16--6.14 points on GeoFWI3D.
The geological and medical studies retain their own computational
allowances and training recipes.

MedSAM3 is adapted for 200 updates using five labelled crops on MSD and
two on ADNI. The highest-mean configurations reach 81.73\% Dice on MSD
and 80.85\% Dice on ADNI,
improving their matched baselines by 5.39 and 3.53 percentage points,
respectively. SAM3 extends pretrained adaptation to salt segmentation,
starting from its general checkpoint and using five labelled volumes
and 25 updates. Its highest-mean configuration reaches 72.76\% Dice,
improving the matched baseline by 3.45 percentage points, with positive
changes in eight of nine comparisons.

The findings characterize performance within the specified budgets.
An annotation audit also identifies
counterexamples to strict geometric proxies for the MSD anatomical
labelling rules, motivating supervision grounded in the stored masks.
Together, the experiments assess the value and limits of explicit spatial
supervision across tasks and learning conditions, with independent
confirmation required to establish broader generality.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
\label{sec:motivation}\label{sec:research-questions}

Three-dimensional semantic segmentation turns an image volume into labelled
regions. In medical imaging, those regions may be organs or anatomical
subdivisions; in geology, they may be formations within a subsurface model.
Their usefulness depends on recovering both the correct voxel labels and
the spatial extent of the structures they represent. A small error near a
boundary can change a measured volume, while an exchange between adjacent
classes can preserve the overall foreground and still produce an incorrect
segmentation.

A reference mask contains information about these relationships. It
identifies the interior and exterior of a region, specifies where labels
change between neighbours, and provides a geometry against which errors
can be measured. Conventional segmentation losses encourage agreement
with the mask, but give these properties different and often implicit
roles in learning. The question addressed here is whether making selected
spatial properties explicit helps a model use its training annotations
more effectively.

This question is especially relevant when annotation is scarce. Medical
segmentation can require specialist interpretation and careful delineation
across many slices, while uncertain boundaries make consistent labelling
difficult \cite{wang2021annotation}. An auxiliary objective derived from
the available masks adds a form of supervision without requiring another
collection of manually annotated images. Its practical value depends on
whether that additional emphasis improves predictions on unseen examples.

Training computation is a second constraint. An objective may accelerate
early learning without improving the performance reached after a much
longer run. Across architectures, an equal number of epochs or updates
also entails different amounts of arithmetic work. The main experiment
therefore measures accuracy under a common optimization FLOP ceiling.
The cost of an active constraint is charged within that allowance, and
its calibration cost is reported separately. The medical comparisons apply this resource definition on both MSD
and ADNI. Pretrained adaptation uses separate fixed schedules: 200 updates
for MedSAM3 on the medical datasets and 25 for SAM3 on GeoFWI3D.

The study begins with salt-body segmentation in GeoFWI3D, whose synthetic
reflectivity volumes provide a nonmedical application of spatial
supervision \cite{swaminadhan2026geofwi3d}. Its main medical application
is hippocampus segmentation in MRI. The hippocampus supports memory and
is affected early in Alzheimer's disease, making its delineation relevant
to quantitative studies of brain structure \cite{niaAlzheimerBrain}.
MSD distinguishes anterior and posterior hippocampus from background
\cite{simpson2019dataset}; it therefore requires both external delineation
and an internal class assignment. The ADNI dataset supplies
a second medical task with a single hippocampus foreground class in
separate left and right crops.

Logic Tensor Networks and logLTN provide the formal language for the
proposed supervision \cite{badreddine2022logic,badreddine2023logltn}.
Four families are developed: membership in bands around a reference
boundary, agreement with neighbouring label transitions, classwise
signed-distance penalties, and class supervision balanced across depth
strata. Each formulation states what is supervised, where it is evaluated
and how its satisfaction becomes a loss. An accompanying annotation audit
examines whether candidate geometric interpretations of anatomical rules
are compatible with the stored MSD labels.

The principal comparisons evaluate nnU-Net, SwinUNETR, UNETR and a compact
3D U-Net under fourteen objectives on GeoFWI3D and both medical datasets,
with three folds and three seeds in each setting. The second strand
adapts pretrained models through low-rank parameters while retaining
their base weights. MedSAM3 continues its released medical adapter using
five labelled crops on MSD and two on ADNI for 200 updates
\cite{liu2025medsam3}. SAM3 starts from its general checkpoint with fresh
adapters and uses five geological volumes for 25 updates
\cite{carion2025sam3}. Fixed text queries identify the target structures
without supplying case-specific localization. These studies examine both
the quality attainable with limited adaptation and the additional effect
of spatial supervision. Each objective is compared with its own matched
baseline under the same initialization, support set and schedule.

The thesis makes three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A common LTN/logLTN formulation of four spatial objectives, with
explicit links between logical statements and implemented training losses.
\item A paired experimental analysis of individual and combined objectives,
including four-model comparisons on three datasets under explicitly
defined FLOP ceilings and an audit of candidate rules against MSD annotations.
\item An evaluation of limited-data adaptation with MedSAM3 on two medical
datasets and SAM3 on geological volumes, assessing the attainable
segmentation quality and the paired contribution of spatial supervision.
\end{enumerate}

The experimental evaluation comprises six completed studies: three conventional
dataset comparisons, two MedSAM3 adaptations and one SAM3 adaptation.
Each includes the full fourteen-objective comparison. The analysis
reports final-budget accuracy, paired changes and variation across the
evaluated folds and seeds. It does not establish that short-budget gains
persist after convergence. Figure~\ref{fig:thesis-overview} summarizes
the relationship between the applications and learning settings.

\section{Background and Related Work}\label{sec:background}

Spatial supervision combines a segmentation model with a rule for comparing
its predictions to annotation-derived geometry. This chapter introduces
the probability representation, conventional objectives and model families
used in that comparison, then develops the logical vocabulary and relevant
prior work. Experiment-specific settings are collected in
Chapter~\ref{sec:methodology}.

\subsection{Segmentation and Supervised Objectives}
\label{sec:volumetric-segmentation}\label{sec:segmentation-objectives}

For case $i$, let
$\Omega_i=\{0,\ldots,D_i-1\}\times\{0,\ldots,H_i-1\}\times
\{0,\ldots,W_i-1\}$ be the voxel grid. The image
$x_i:\Omega_i\to\mathbb R^M$ contains $M$ measurements at each location,
and the reference map $y_i:\Omega_i\to\mathcal C$ assigns one of $K$
mutually exclusive classes. Background has label zero. The one-hot value
$y_{i,c}(v)=\mathbf1\{y_i(v)=c\}$ indicates whether voxel $v$ belongs to
class $c$. A network with parameters $\theta$ produces logits
$z_{\theta,i,c}(v)$, converted to probabilities by
\begin{equation}
p_{\theta,i,c}(v)=
\frac{\exp(z_{\theta,i,c}(v))}
{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{C}}\exp(z_{\theta,i,k}(v))},
\qquad
\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}p_{\theta,i,c}(v)=1.
\label{eq:segmentation-softmax}
\end{equation}
Training operates on these continuous outputs. The final label is
$\widehat y_i(v)=\operatorname{arg\,max}_{c\in\mathcal C}
p_{\theta,i,c}(v)$. The same notation covers predictions assembled from
two-dimensional slices once their outputs have been converted to a common
semantic probability field.

The class vocabulary determines which errors a constraint can detect.
Salt segmentation uses $\mathcal C=\{0,S\}$ and binary hippocampus
segmentation uses $\{0,H\}$. MSD instead uses $\{0,A,P\}$ for background,
anterior and posterior hippocampus. Its whole-foreground probability is
$q_i=p_{\theta,i,A}+p_{\theta,i,P}$. A term depending only on this sum
cannot distinguish an anterior/posterior exchange that preserves it.
Furthermore, foreground thresholding and multiclass argmax are distinct
decision rules: for $(p_0,p_A,p_P)=(0.40,0.35,0.25)$, the foreground sum
exceeds $0.5$ although background is the most probable individual class.
The experiments use multiclass argmax before evaluating the labelled
regions.

Cross-entropy penalizes low probability for the reference class:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{\mathrm{CE},i}
&=-\frac{1}{|\Omega_i|}\sum_{v\in\Omega_i}
  \sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}y_{i,c}(v)\log p_{\theta,i,c}(v)\\
&=-\frac{1}{|\Omega_i|}\sum_{v\in\Omega_i}
  \log p_{\theta,i,y_i(v)}(v).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cross-entropy}
\end{equation}
Each location contributes a class error, while the network can use spatial
context to determine its prediction. The voxelwise reduction therefore
does not assume independent image locations. Fixed nonnegative weights
with $\sum_v w_i(v)=1$ give the weighted form
$-\sum_v w_i(v)\log p_{\theta,i,y_i(v)}(v)$. This changes the allocation
of supervision across the volume and is the basis of the depth objective.

Dice-based losses assess regional overlap \cite{milletari2016vnet}.
The linear-denominator soft Dice used in this study is
\begin{equation}
D_{i,c}^{\mathrm{soft}}=
\frac{2\sum_{v\in\Omega_i}p_{\theta,i,c}(v)y_{i,c}(v)+\varepsilon}
     {\sum_{v\in\Omega_i}p_{\theta,i,c}(v)
      +\sum_{v\in\Omega_i}y_{i,c}(v)+\varepsilon},
\qquad \varepsilon>0.
\label{eq:soft-dice}
\end{equation}
For a selected class set $\mathcal C_D$, the corresponding loss is
$1-|\mathcal C_D|^{-1}\sum_{c\in\mathcal C_D}D^{\mathrm{soft}}_{i,c}$.
Unlike a sum of local class errors, the ratio couples predictions through
the total predicted and reference region sizes. Whether background enters
the class average, whether region sums are pooled across cases, and which
smoothing constant is used are consequently part of the loss definition.
Evaluation Dice uses discrete regions and is specified separately in
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-methods}.

Dice and cross-entropy can be combined to supply regional and local class
supervision. Neither explicitly weights an error by its distance from a
reference interface or compares predicted and annotated changes across
a neighbouring pair. The auxiliary objectives introduce these forms of
agreement. Their effect must be interpreted relative to the supervised
loss already present, particularly when an added term itself contains
cross-entropy.

Geometry also requires a coordinate convention. Voxel indices are mapped
to physical positions by
\begin{equation}
r_i(v)=Q_i v+b_i,
\qquad
d_i^{\mathrm{phys}}(u,v)=\lVert Q_i(u-v)\rVert_2.
\label{eq:physical-coordinates}
\end{equation}
where $Q_i$ represents orientation and scaling and $b_i$ the origin.
NIfTI headers store coordinate transforms and spatial units
\cite{nifti1fields}. A displacement of one voxel can thus have different
physical lengths along different axes. The face-sharing voxel graph is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}_i=\{\{u,v\}:u,v\in\Omega_i,\ \lVert u-v\rVert_1=1\}.
\label{eq:voxel-neighbourhood}
\end{equation}
and gives each interior voxel six neighbours. Bands and edges below use
this grid geometry. Depth weighting uses physical coordinates, so the
validity of the image transform matters directly to its construction.

\subsection{Segmentation Architectures}\label{sec:segmentation-architectures}

The four architectures trained from scratch all connect an encoder to a
decoder that restores voxelwise predictions. Their encoders construct
context in different ways. Comparing them tests whether the same
output-level spatial objective remains useful when the underlying
representation and cost of optimization change.

U-Net builds increasingly coarse feature maps through an encoder and
combines them with an upsampling decoder through skip connections
\cite{ronneberger2015unet}. Coarse features provide context, while the
skips retain information useful for localization. Three-dimensional
U-Net applies these operations across a volume \cite{cicek2016unet}.
The compact implementation in this study provides a convolutional
reference with a relatively small configured network.

nnU-Net uses a U-Net architecture within a system that configures
preprocessing, network design and training for the segmentation problem
\cite{isensee2021nnunet}. Its identity therefore includes the training
pipeline as well as the encoder. The present experiment fixes a particular
configuration and retains its native supervised loss and deep supervision.
This makes the constrained and unconstrained versions comparable within
that system.

Transformer encoders use self-attention to combine token features through
input-dependent query--key similarities \cite{vaswani2017attention}.
UNETR divides the volume into patches, embeds them as a sequence and
applies global attention on a fixed token grid
\cite{hatamizadeh2021unetr}. Features from intermediate blocks are
reshaped and upsampled for a convolutional decoder. Attention can connect
distant patches within the supplied crop, although the crop itself still
limits the available context.

SwinUNETR forms a hierarchy of transformer features
\cite{hatamizadeh2022swin}. Attention is restricted to local windows;
alternating shifted partitions exchange information across their
boundaries, and patch merging produces coarser representations
\cite{liu2021swin}. The resulting feature maps feed a convolutional
decoder. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture-families} contrasts this hierarchy
with UNETR's fixed token grid and the convolutional U-Net organization.

These architectural differences also affect the meaning of a training
budget. Parameter count describes stored weights, whereas computational
cost depends additionally on feature-map dimensions, kernel reuse, token
counts and decoder operations. Windowed attention reduces pairwise
interaction costs at a fixed token count, but does not alone determine
the cost of a complete model. The experiments therefore profile the
configured models and objectives; their widths and optimizers are given
in Section~\ref{sec:main-msd-methods}.

\subsection{Logic Tensor Networks and logLTN}\label{sec:ltn-background}

Logic Tensor Networks associate first-order expressions with numerical
groundings that can be differentiated during learning
\cite{badreddine2022logic}. Individuals are represented by constants or
variables, predicates evaluate their properties in $[0,1]$, and numerical
operators combine these truth degrees. A segmentation predicate can take
a voxel and its image as an individual and use the model's class
probability as its grounding:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}\bigl(\mathsf{Seg}_c(i,v)\bigr)
=p_{\theta,i,c}(v).
\label{eq:segmentation-predicate}
\end{equation}
Reference indicators and annotation-derived geometry provide fixed
information during an update. The network supplies the trainable values.
The satisfaction of a compound statement depends on the selected logical
operators; it need not be a calibrated probability of the statement as
a whole.

logLTN carries out the corresponding operations in logarithmic truth
space \cite{badreddine2023logltn}. If
$\ell_\theta(\phi)=\log\mathcal G_\theta(\phi)$, complete satisfaction
has value zero and lower satisfaction has a negative value. Product
conjunction becomes addition. For the objectives in this thesis,
universal reduction takes the weighted mean of local log-truths:
\begin{equation}
\ell_{\theta}(\forall_{\omega}u\in U:\,P(u))
=\sum_{u\in U}\omega(u)\ell_{\theta}(P(u)),
\qquad
\omega(u)>0,\quad\sum_{u\in U}\omega(u)=1.
\label{eq:weighted-log-universal}
\end{equation}
Uniform weights yield the ordinary mean; exponentiation then gives the
geometric mean of the local truths. This normalization prevents a larger
domain from increasing the loss solely because it contains more
individuals. The particular weights and domain still determine how
strongly each location contributes.

For example, requiring foreground membership on an annotated inner band
means aggregating $\log q_F(v)$ over its selected voxels, where
$q_F=\sum_{c\in F}p_c$ for a class group $F$ with nonempty complement.
On an outer band, the negated statement contributes $\log(1-q_F(v))$.
The sum defining $q_F$ follows the mutually exclusive class vocabulary.
Using a fuzzy disjunction instead, or replacing a restricted domain by
an implication over the full grid, would generally define a different
objective. Stable logit-based evaluation is given in
Appendix~\ref{sec:technical-details}.

Geometric errors can enter the same framework. Any nonnegative violation
$e_\theta(u)$ defines an agreement predicate through
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}(\mathsf{Cons}(u))=\exp(-e_{\theta}(u)),
\qquad
\ell_{\theta}(\mathsf{Cons}(u))=-e_{\theta}(u).
\label{eq:penalty-predicate}
\end{equation}
The negative mean log-truth is then exactly the mean violation. The
implementation evaluates that violation directly; it need not compute
an exponential and subsequently its logarithm. This provides a common
description of probability-based membership and geometric penalties.

An exact correspondence requires the same groundings, domains, weights
and aggregation order in the logical expression and the implemented loss.
When those operations agree, so do their gradients. Logical notation
therefore clarifies what the model is asked to satisfy; empirical
comparisons establish whether that supervision helps. These constraints
remain soft objectives and do not guarantee satisfaction on the final
discrete prediction.

\subsection{Related Work on Spatial Supervision}\label{sec:spatial-related-work}

Prior work incorporates spatial information through location-dependent
weights, geometric penalties and representations of valid shape.
Ronneberger et al.\ weighted cross-entropy near boundaries separating
touching cells \cite{ronneberger2015unet}. This demonstrates how an
annotation can specify where class supervision deserves extra emphasis.
The boundary bands studied here use explicit inner and outer domains,
with balanced side reductions. In the principal experiment, those domains
follow the exterior of the whole foreground.

Kervadec et al.\ formulate boundary supervision as a regional integral
of predicted probabilities against a reference signed-distance field
\cite{kervadec2019boundary}. The formulation supplies a geometric
learning signal without extracting a hard predicted contour. The
classwise distance term in this thesis follows that approach, with
specified discrete distances, clipping and normalization. A constant
shift makes its loss nonnegative while preserving the model gradient.

Anatomically Constrained Neural Networks supervise segmentation through
a learned representation of valid shapes \cite{oktay2017anatomically}.
clDice instead uses mask and skeleton overlap to address connectivity
in tubular structures \cite{shit2021cldice}. These approaches concern
properties that local membership and transition agreement do not enforce.
The present edge term preserves the changes in the annotation, including
its boundaries; it does not require a globally connected prediction.

Bergamin et al.\ use LTNs for MSD hippocampus segmentation with relations
concerning connection, non-nesting and relative volume
\cite{bergamin2025integrating}. Their work provides a direct predecessor
for logical supervision of this task. The present study evaluates four
families of local or classwise geometric supervision across multiple
architectures under an optimization FLOP ceiling. It also examines the
compatibility of candidate anatomical proxies with the actual label maps
(Section~\ref{sec:annotation-results}).

Together, these studies motivate explicit spatial supervision while
showing that different objectives encode different properties. The
fourteen-objective comparison evaluates the selected families and their
combinations; the literature methods provide context rather than further
experimental arms. Differences in datasets and protocols preclude a
numerical ranking against those published systems.

\subsection{Pretrained Segmentation and Low-Rank Adaptation}
\label{sec:pretrained-adaptation}

Pretraining changes the starting point of learning. A target model can
specialize representations acquired from a larger training collection,
so the relevant resource question becomes the additional data and
optimization needed for adaptation. Prompting specifies the target of
an individual prediction; fine-tuning changes parameters using labelled
examples. A pretrained segmentation system can use both.

SAM combines an image encoder, a prompt encoder and a mask decoder,
using prompts such as points or bounding boxes
\cite{kirillov2023segment}. MedSAM adapts this framework to medical
images with box prompts \cite{ma2024medsam}. SAM3 supports concepts
specified through text or exemplars \cite{carion2025sam3}, and MedSAM3
specializes that capability for medical concepts \cite{liu2025medsam3}.
The hippocampus experiments use fixed anatomical phrases, and the
geological SAM3 experiment uses the phrase \emph{salt body}. These queries
are shared across cases; inference receives no case-specific localization
from a held-out reference mask.

Low-Rank Adaptation parameterizes the update to a selected weight matrix
through two smaller trainable factors \cite{hu2021lora}:
\begin{equation}
W_{\mathrm{eff}}=W_0+\frac{\alpha}{r}BA,
\qquad
A\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}},\quad
B\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r},
\label{eq:lora-weight-update}
\end{equation}
The base matrix $W_0$ is frozen, $r$ is the rank and $\alpha$ controls
the update scale. The factors contain
$r(d_{\mathrm{in}}+d_{\mathrm{out}})$ parameters, compared with
$d_{\mathrm{in}}d_{\mathrm{out}}$ in the dense matrix. The rank restriction
applies to the update, while the pretrained matrix remains available
in the forward computation.

Optimizing fewer parameters reduces gradient and optimizer-state storage.
It still requires execution of the underlying model and propagation of
gradients to the adapter locations. Parameter count, memory and runtime
therefore describe different aspects of adaptation effort. MedSAM3
continues a released medical adapter, whereas SAM3 starts with fresh
factors whose initial product $BA$ is zero. Both studies optimize
18,497,664 adapter parameters and restore the same starting state before
each matched run. Their initial representations differ, while their
paired comparisons isolate the effect of the training objective within
each study. Sections~\ref{sec:medsam-methods} and~\ref{sec:sam3-methods}
specify the semantic outputs, support data and adaptation schedules.

\section{Spatial Constraints in the LTN/logLTN Framework}
\label{sec:spatial-constraints}

The four objectives developed here extract different learning signals from
the same reference mask. Bands emphasize membership near its boundary;
edges compare neighbouring transitions; signed distances describe errors
relative to individual classes; and depth weights redistribute class
supervision across spatial strata. Each section introduces the reason
for the construction, defines its numerical form and explains the errors
to which it responds. Calibration and training schedules are specified
in Chapter~\ref{sec:methodology}; supporting derivations appear in
Appendix~\ref{sec:technical-details}.

\subsection{Shared Formulation}\label{sec:constraint-formulation}

Let $F$ be a nonempty subset of the class vocabulary with a nonempty
complement. Its reference membership and predicted probability are
\begin{equation}
h_{i,F}(v)=\sum_{c\in F}y_{i,c}(v),
\qquad
q_{\theta,i,F}(v)=\sum_{c\in F}p_{\theta,i,c}(v).
\label{eq:constraint-group-membership}
\end{equation}
The reference region is $M_{i,F}=\{v\in\Omega_i:h_{i,F}(v)=1\}$,
and the predicate $\mathsf{In}_F(i,v)$ is grounded by
$q_{\theta,i,F}(v)$. The annotation determines both the membership
target and any spatial supports, distances or weights derived from it.
These quantities are fixed with respect to $\theta$ during an update.
Preprocessing keeps the image, labels and coordinate map consistent.
Where the conventional geological UNETR recipe transforms a training
example, its labels and spatial targets follow the same transformation.

For the principal MSD comparison, bands and edges use the hippocampal
union $F=\{A,P\}$. Signed distance uses separate fields for $A$ and $P$,
while depth supervision retains the correct semantic class at each
voxel. Binary tasks use salt or hippocampus as the foreground group.
MedSAM3 uses a further variant: bands and edges are evaluated separately
for background, anterior and posterior on MSD, so their supports also
follow the internal class interface. Its binary ADNI implementation uses
the hippocampus membership predicate. SAM3 on GeoFWI3D likewise uses a
single salt-membership predicate, with full-volume supports evaluated
through sampled planes. Figure~\ref{fig:constraint-domains}
illustrates the distinction between voxel, pair, distance and weighted
class supervision.

The common log-space formulation turns each local truth into a loss
through its negative log-grounding. Membership contributes a
log-probability; a nonnegative geometric error contributes its negative
through an exponential agreement predicate. Domain selection and
aggregation complete the definition. In particular, a mean over voxels
followed by a mean over cases differs from pooling all voxels first.
The reductions below retain the conventions of the principal
implementation, including the treatment of empty supports.

All four families use the geometry actually present in the annotation.
They therefore remain applicable when a simplified anatomical proxy
fails to reproduce that geometry. The separate MSD audit in
Sections~\ref{sec:annotation-compatibility} and~\ref{sec:annotation-results}
examines this issue for proposed anatomical A/P transition rules.

\subsection{Boundary-Band Membership}\label{sec:boundary-band-constraints}

Boundary bands concentrate membership supervision on the two sides of an
annotated interface. An inner band encourages the prediction to retain
foreground close to that interface, while an outer band discourages
extension into adjacent background. The selected locations depend on the
reference mask, so an incorrect predicted contour cannot move the target
support away from an error.

For the six-neighbour structuring element
$\mathcal N_6=\{0,\pm e_1,\pm e_2,\pm e_3\}$, where $e_k$ denotes
a unit array offset, dilation and erosion are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Dil}_6(M)
 &=\{v\in\Omega_i:\exists a\in\mathcal{N}_6,\ v+a\in M\},\\
\operatorname{Ero}_6(M)
 &=\{v\in\Omega_i:\forall a\in\mathcal{N}_6,\ v+a\in M\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:band-morphology}
\end{equation}
Dilation includes a voxel when at least one location in its neighbourhood
belongs to the region. Erosion retains it only when the complete
neighbourhood belongs to the region. Locations outside $\Omega_i$ are
treated as absent, corresponding to zero padding. Repeating each
operation twice gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
B_{i,F}^{\mathrm{in}}
 &=M_{i,F}\setminus\operatorname{Ero}_6^{\,r}(M_{i,F}),\\
B_{i,F}^{\mathrm{out}}
 &=\operatorname{Dil}_6^{\,r}(M_{i,F})\setminus M_{i,F},
\qquad r=2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:reference-boundary-bands}
\end{equation}
The inner support contains the foreground removed by erosion and the
outer support the background reached by dilation. Their two-step width
is defined on the voxel graph. Narrow parts of the region can belong
entirely to the inner band. At a crop face, zero padding can likewise
place foreground in the inner band, whereas the outer support remains
limited to observed voxels.

The corresponding logical statements require positive membership on
the inner support and its negation on the outer support:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_i^{\mathrm{in}}
 &=\forall v\in B_i^{\mathrm{in}}:\,\mathsf{In}_{F}(i,v),\\
\Phi_i^{\mathrm{out}}
 &=\forall v\in B_i^{\mathrm{out}}:\,\lnot\mathsf{In}_{F}(i,v).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:band-logical-statements}
\end{equation}
For nonempty sides with $n_i^s=|B_i^s|$, the implemented loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{B,i}=-\frac12\left[
\frac{\sum_{v\in B_i^{\mathrm{in}}}\log q_i(v)}{n_i^{\mathrm{in}}+\varepsilon}
+\frac{\sum_{v\in B_i^{\mathrm{out}}}\log(1-q_i(v))}{n_i^{\mathrm{out}}+\varepsilon}
\right],\qquad \varepsilon=10^{-6}.
\label{eq:balanced-band-loss}
\end{equation}
Reducing each side separately gives them equal nominal weight even when
their voxel counts differ. For example, doubling the number of outer
voxels with the same mean prediction error does not double that side's
contribution. The denominator adjustment retains the factor
$n/(n+\varepsilon)$ in each mean; its exact satisfaction interpretation
is recorded in Appendix~\ref{sec:technical-details}.

The main MSD implementation averages case losses only when both bands
are nonempty. If no case meets that condition, it returns zero connected
to the prediction graph. Empty and entirely foreground grids consequently
receive no band penalty, while retaining their supervised baseline loss.
The local calculation is ordinary binary cross-entropy from grouped
float32 logits; focal and degree-based weights are disabled.

Gradient descent raises foreground membership on inner voxels and lowers
it on outer voxels. The term provides no direct supervision outside
these bands, including at remote false positives. For the MSD union,
it also cannot resolve an anterior/posterior exchange that leaves the
foreground probability unchanged. These properties make bands a focused
addition to the full segmentation objective rather than a complete
description of segmentation accuracy.

\subsection{Edge Consistency}\label{sec:edge-constraints}

Membership supervision treats each selected voxel separately. The edge
objective adds a relation between predictions at neighbouring locations:
their difference should match the difference in the reference labels.
The domain contains face-sharing pairs whose endpoints both lie in the
combined band support,
\begin{equation}
S_{i,F}^{B}=B_{i,F}^{\mathrm{in}}\cup B_{i,F}^{\mathrm{out}},
\qquad
\mathcal{E}_{i,F}^{B}
=\bigl\{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal{E}_i:
          u,v\in S_{i,F}^{B}\bigr\}.
\label{eq:edge-supported-pairs}
\end{equation}
with each undirected pair counted once through positive offsets along
the array axes. Pairs crossing a crop face are absent. Every selected
pair has the same weight, without a correction for physical spacing or
face area.

Write $\delta_i(v)=q_i(v)-h_i(v)$ for the membership residual. The
transition discrepancy and its squared penalty are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
d_i(u,v)
 &=\bigl(q_i(u)-q_i(v)\bigr)
   -\bigl(h_i(u)-h_i(v)\bigr)\\
 &=\delta_i(u)-\delta_i(v),
\qquad e_i^{E}(u,v)=d_i(u,v)^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:edge-transition-discrepancy}
\end{equation}
Equal reference labels have target difference zero. Across an annotated
boundary, the signed target is $+1$ or $-1$. The direction matters: a
reference pair $(1,0)$ predicted as $(0,1)$ has squared discrepancy four,
even though the absolute contrast is correct. This construction preserves
the annotated transition as well as encouraging agreement within each
side.

An exponential grounding expresses transition agreement as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}\bigl(\mathsf{Transition}_{F}(i,u,v)\bigr)
 &=\exp\bigl(-e_i^E(u,v)\bigr),\\
\Phi_i^E
 &=\forall\{u,v\}\in\mathcal{E}_i^B:
     \mathsf{Transition}_{F}(i,u,v).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:edge-logical-statement}
\end{equation}
and its negative mean log-truth gives
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{E,i}
=-\ell_{\theta}(\Phi_i^E)
=\frac{1}{n_i^E}\sum_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal{E}_i^B}
   \bigl(\delta_i(u)-\delta_i(v)\bigr)^2.
\label{eq:edge-case-loss}
\end{equation}
for $n_i^E=|\mathcal E_i^B|>0$. Pair counts from all three directions
are pooled before dividing. This gives every pair equal influence,
whereas averaging three directional means would give different pair
weights when counts differ. There is no count stabilizer. The batch
mean includes cases with at least one valid pair and returns a
differentiable zero when none remains.

The residual form explains the relationship to bands. An edge loss of
zero requires $\delta_i$ to be constant within each connected component
of the selected pair graph. A component with only one reference label
can therefore retain a uniform membership error. If it contains both
zero and one labels, the bounds $0\leq q_i\leq1$ force this constant
to zero. Bands directly anchor individual memberships; edges constrain
how those memberships change locally.

The sigmoid used to obtain membership probabilities attenuates the
logit gradient near saturation, as derived in
Appendix~\ref{sec:technical-details}. The selected graph also covers
only a local band region. Its consistency does not establish global
connectivity or correct distant predictions. As with union bands,
anterior/posterior exchanges preserving foreground membership remain
outside the direct supervision of the main MSD edge term.

\subsection{Classwise Signed Distance}\label{sec:sdf-constraints}

A distance field gives prediction errors a geometric scale over the whole
volume. Constructing a separate field for each foreground class also
makes the internal anterior/posterior interface visible to the loss.
For $M_{i,c}=\{v\in\Omega_i:y_i(v)=c\}$, define
$\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{vox}}(v,U)=\min_{u\in U}\|v-u\|_2$
on the unit voxel grid. When the class and its complement are nonempty,
the signed field is
\begin{equation}
\widetilde\varphi_{i,c}(v)=
\begin{cases}
-\bigl(\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{vox}}
       (v,\Omega_i\setminus M_{i,c})-\tfrac12\bigr),
   &v\in M_{i,c},\\[0.4em]
\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{vox}}(v,M_{i,c})-\tfrac12,
   &v\notin M_{i,c}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:sdf-half-voxel-field}
\end{equation}
It is negative inside the class and positive outside. The half-voxel
shift places adjacent inside and outside centres on opposite sides of
the interface. This defines a discrete distance convention; it is not
an exact continuous-surface distance for every boundary orientation.
Clipping and normalization give
\begin{equation}
\varphi_{i,c}(v)
=\frac{\operatorname{clip}
  (\widetilde\varphi_{i,c}(v),-\tau,\tau)}{\tau},
\qquad \tau=8.
\label{eq:sdf-clipped-normalized-field}
\end{equation}
so geometric weights are bounded. An absent class is assigned $+1$
throughout the grid and a full-grid class $-1$; both are retained in
the class reduction. The field uses the stored grid without adding an
exterior background frame.

The local penalty combines class error and signed distance:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
e_{i,c}(v)
&=\bigl(p_{i,c}(v)-y_{i,c}(v)\bigr)\varphi_{i,c}(v)\\
&=\begin{cases}
   (1-p_{i,c}(v))\,|\varphi_{i,c}(v)|, &y_{i,c}(v)=1,\\
   p_{i,c}(v)\,|\varphi_{i,c}(v)|, &y_{i,c}(v)=0.
  \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sdf-local-violation}
\end{equation}
The two cases show its meaning directly. Inside the reference region,
the penalty increases with missed class probability; outside, it
increases with excess class probability. In both cases the distance
magnitude weights the error, making the product nonnegative.
The grounding
$\mathcal G_\theta(\mathsf{Geom}_c(i,v))=\exp(-e_{i,c}(v))$
and statement
$\Psi_{i,c}=\forall v\in\Omega_i:\mathsf{Geom}_c(i,v)$
therefore yield the MSD loss
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SDF},i}
&=-\frac12\sum_{c\in\{A,P\}}\ell_\theta(\Psi_{i,c})\\
&=\frac{1}{2|\Omega_i|}
  \sum_{c\in\{A,P\}}\sum_{v\in\Omega_i}
  \bigl(p_{i,c}(v)-y_{i,c}(v)\bigr)\varphi_{i,c}(v).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sdf-full-volume-loss}
\end{equation}
Case losses are averaged over the batch. Each foreground class and each
stored voxel has equal weight in this reduction, including crop-face
voxels; foreground and background are not balanced separately within
a class. Distances are constructed from the transformed reference grid
used by the current prediction.

This loss follows the distance-based boundary formulation of
Kervadec et al.\ \cite{kervadec2019boundary}. Relative to its regional
probability--distance term, subtracting
$(2|\Omega_i|)^{-1}\sum_{c,v}y_{i,c}(v)\varphi_{i,c}(v)$
adds only a reference-dependent shift. It makes the objective
nonnegative without altering the model gradient, allowing an exact
agreement-predicate interpretation.

Compared with bands, the spatial reach is broader. A misplaced class
probability farther from its interface receives a larger geometric
weight until the eight-voxel clipping threshold, beyond which that
weight remains constant. Separate $A$ and $P$ fields penalize internal
class exchanges even when the whole foreground is correct. This is
geometric error supervision, with no guarantee of a particular final
topology.

\subsection{Depth-Weighted Class Supervision}\label{sec:depth-constraints}

Ordinary voxel averaging allocates more total supervision to spatial
regions containing more voxels. Depth weighting instead divides a
region around the hippocampus into strata and controls their relative
contributions. The annotation determines this spatial coordinate, while
the target remains the original semantic class. The construction thus
supervises anterior and posterior labels without identifying their
interface with a universal separating plane.

Let $\mu_i$ be the foreground centroid in physical
right--anterior--superior coordinates and $\ell_i$ its dominant unit
principal axis. Project the rightward unit vector $e_R$ perpendicular
to that axis:
\begin{equation}
\widetilde n_i=e_R-(e_R^{\mathsf T}\ell_i)\ell_i,
\qquad
n_i=\frac{\widetilde n_i}{\|\widetilde n_i\|_2}.
\label{eq:depth-plane-normal}
\end{equation}
The normal defines a plane through the centroid containing the
longitudinal direction, and signed distance to it gives depth:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Pi_i&=\{r\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:n_i^{\mathsf T}(r-\mu_i)=0\},\\
\eta_i(v)&=n_i^{\mathsf T}(r_i(v)-\mu_i).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:depth-plane-coordinate}
\end{equation}
This coordinate measures distance across the longitudinal plane in
millimetres. It is distinct from an array slice index or a position
along the long axis. At least four foreground points are required,
and the implementation rejects a nearly degenerate projection when
$\|\widetilde n_i\|_2<0.1$.

The supervised region $R_i$ contains the foreground and background
within $8\,\mathrm{mm}$ of it, clipped to the stored volume. Consecutive
signed strata of width $\Delta=2\,\mathrm{mm}$ are defined by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_i(v)&=\left\lfloor\frac{\eta_i(v)}{\Delta}\right\rfloor,\\
D_{i,b}&=\{v\in R_i:\kappa_i(v)=b\},
\qquad N_{i,b}=|D_{i,b}|,\\
\mathcal{B}_i&=\{b\in\mathbb{Z}:N_{i,b}>0\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:depth-strata}
\end{equation}
The floor operation keeps the two sides of the plane separate.
Each stratum may contain several semantic classes, whose reference
labels remain unchanged.

Assigning equal mass to every occupied stratum could give excessive
influence to a very sparse one. The implemented rule instead uses
\begin{equation}
 m_{i,b}=\min\left(\frac{N_{i,b}}{T},1\right),
\qquad
 Z_i^{\mathrm{depth}}=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}_i}m_{i,b},
\qquad
 \alpha_{i,b}=\frac{m_{i,b}}{Z_i^{\mathrm{depth}}}.
\label{eq:depth-stratum-masses}
\end{equation}
with $T=64$ voxels, and spreads each normalized mass uniformly across
its stratum:
\begin{equation}
 w_i(v)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{i,\kappa_i(v)}}{N_{i,\kappa_i(v)}},
       &v\in R_i,\\[0.6em]
 0,    &v\notin R_i.
 \end{cases}
\label{eq:depth-voxel-weights}
\end{equation}
The weights sum to one. Strata with at least 64 voxels receive equal
total mass; smaller strata receive proportionally less. As an
illustration, three strata of 32, 128 and 256 voxels receive total
weights $0.2$, $0.4$ and $0.4$. The two larger strata contribute
equally despite their different sizes, while the sparse stratum is
partially downweighted. These numbers illustrate the rule rather
than an observed dataset distribution.

With $\mathsf{Correct}(i,v)$ grounded by
$p_{\theta,i,y_i(v)}(v)$, the weighted statement
$\Phi_i^{\mathrm{depth}}=\forall_{w_i}v\in R_i:
\mathsf{Correct}(i,v)$ has loss
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{depth},i}
 &=-\ell_\theta(\Phi_i^{\mathrm{depth}})\\
 &=-\sum_{v\in R_i}w_i(v)\log p_{\theta,i,y_i(v)}(v)\\
 &=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}_i}\alpha_{i,b}
   \left[-\frac{1}{N_{i,b}}\sum_{v\in D_{i,b}}
                    \log p_{\theta,i,y_i(v)}(v)\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:depth-weighted-class-loss}
\end{equation}
The last expression shows a weighted average of stratum-wise
cross-entropies. Depth determines where supervision receives weight;
it adds neither a focal factor nor a separate class weight, and it
does not multiply the signed-distance loss.

Physical geometry is essential to this definition. The distance
transform uses axis spacings and assumes orthogonal voxel axes,
so sheared affines are rejected. Coordinate transformations must
preserve the relation between labels and their coordinate map.
The principal MSD recipe evaluates the term on the primary grid
with one volume per update. Further coordinate and centroid details
are provided in Appendix~\ref{sec:technical-details}.

\subsection{Combined Objective and Experimental Scope}
\label{sec:constraint-combination}

For configuration $a$ with selected families $J_a$, training minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{a}^{(t)}(\theta)
=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{seg},a}(\theta)
+\sum_{j\in J_a}\lambda_{a,j}\,s_{a,j}(t)\,
\mathcal{L}_{a,j}(\theta),
\qquad \lambda_{a,j}\geq0,\quad 0\leq s_{a,j}(t)\leq1.
\label{eq:scheduled-constraint-objective}
\end{equation}
at update $t$. The coefficient $\lambda_{a,j}$ sets a term's scale,
while $s_{a,j}(t)$ determines when it becomes active. Both are fixed
when differentiating that update. If
$S_{a,j}=\exp(-\mathcal L_{a,j})$, the auxiliary sum is the negative
logarithm of
$\prod_{j\in J_a}S_{a,j}^{\lambda_{a,j}s_{a,j}(t)}$.
The supervised baseline retains its native convention, including
nnU-Net's negative Dice term.

Complementary domains motivate combinations, but do not ensure
complementary gradients. Terms can reinforce or oppose one another,
and their computation consumes part of a fixed optimization allowance.
The experiments therefore treat coefficients, activation schedules
and resource accounting as parts of the tested configuration,
specified in Chapter~\ref{sec:methodology}.

Task-specific variants also retain their own domains and reductions.
GeoFWI3D uses binary salt membership and one foreground distance field.
Its conventional UNETR recipe excludes an observation frame from bands
and edges as a modification introduced to improve geological
generalization (Section~\ref{sec:geo-methods}); the other three
conventional recipes retain crop-face support. MedSAM3 and SAM3 use full-volume normalization while sampling
image planes: their bands and edges supervise three class masks on MSD,
the hippocampus mask on ADNI and the salt mask on GeoFWI3D. SAM3 retains
zero-padded morphology without observation-frame exclusion. ADNI and
SAM3 depth targets use source-array geometry, with distances in voxels
and no inferred anatomical orientation; MSD uses physical coordinates.
These variations are specified with their protocols. At inference, the
network requires its image and any specified prompts; annotation-derived
constraint targets are used only during training.

\section{Datasets and Experimental Methodology}

The experiments pair each constrained run with a baseline sharing its
model recipe, support set, fold and optimization seed. The medical
four-model studies match optimization FLOPs; the geological four-model

\subsection{Design and Units of Comparison}

initialization and data presentation; changing folds also changes the

\subsection{GeoFWI3D: Salt Segmentation}
\label{sec:geo-methods}

\subsubsection{Images and Reference Masks}

The geological collection is drawn from archive 0 of GeoFWI3D
\cite{swaminadhan2026geofwi3d}, containing 1,000 synthetic scenes on
$96\times96\times96$ grids. The experiment retains 479 scenes with
salt: 249 without faults and 230 with faults. This selection defines
a delineation task conditional on salt being present. Entirely
salt-free scenes are outside its evaluation population.

Synthetic reflectivity is the model input. Each volume is divided by
the 99.5th percentile of its absolute intensities and clipped to
$[-1,1]$, using only image values. Velocity and relative geological
time are used to build the annotation. Inspection showed that zero
relative geological time can also mark null artifacts, so the reference
requires agreement with the scene's recorded salt velocity:
\begin{equation}
 y_i(v)=\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{RGT}_i(v)=0\}
        \mathbf{1}\{V_{P,i}(v)=\operatorname{float32}(V_{\mathrm{salt},i})\}.
\label{eq:geo-reference-construction}
\end{equation}
The equality uses the stored simulation value. All physically valid
salt components are retained, and checks reject physical salt outside
the implicit mask. This correction matters for boundary supervision:
an artifact in the source field would otherwise be treated as a
geological interface.

\subsubsection{Three-Fold, Three-Seed Design}

The completed geological comparison evaluates nnU-Net, SwinUNETR,
UNETR and the compact 3D U-Net under the same fourteen-objective
inventory used in the medical studies. Folds 0, 1 and 2 each contain
19 labelled training scenes and 96 validation scenes on the native
$96^3$ grid. The support set is the frozen approximately 5\% subset
of the eligible training pool. Training and validation are disjoint
within each fold, and the three validation sets are pairwise disjoint.
The support sets contain 54 distinct scenes across their 57 entries.
Seeds 0, 1 and 2 are used in every condition, giving 504 cells.
All nine final-budget outcomes enter each condition mean.

The final matrix combines 36 matched frozen baselines with 468
constrained results. 

\subsubsection{Frozen Model Recipes and Spatial Geometry}

The model recipes preserve batch size one and CUDA bfloat16 autocast
with float32 parameters. nnU-Net uses its native Dice-plus-cross-entropy
objective and deep supervision. SwinUNETR and the compact U-Net use
Dice with background included. Geological UNETR uses foreground
Dice plus cross-entropy, squared prediction terms in Dice, dropout
0.1 and learning rate $6\times10^{-4}$.

In preliminary experiments, the initial UNETR recipe consistently struggled
to achieve satisfactory validation performance across the seeds tested
within the fixed FLOP budget. Two modifications were introduced during
development to improve its generalization on GeoFWI3D: horizontal data
augmentation and exclusion of the observation frame from the spatial
constraints. Both address properties of this geological task and were
retained in its final UNETR training recipe.

The augmentation policy selects among eight transformations in the
horizontal (XY) plane: rotations of $0^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $180^\circ$
or $270^\circ$, each with or without a reflection. Each transformation
is applied jointly to the image and its reference mask, preserving the
depth (Z) axis and their alignment. These operations rearrange voxels
without interpolation or intensity changes and are applied only during
training. The policy is shared by the UNETR baseline and all its
constrained objectives.

The second modification excludes the outer two voxel layers from the
band and edge supports. A salt body can continue beyond the observed
cube, whereas zero-padded erosion can treat the cube face as a region
boundary. Exclusion is intended to concentrate auxiliary supervision
on interfaces observed within the volume, reducing its dependence on
where the observation window ends. It changes the support of the
constraints while preserving the full input, prediction grid and
baseline segmentation loss. Its development comparison is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:geo-results}; the modification is a task-specific
loss-design choice rather than a requirement of the UNETR architecture.

All four models use two-step, six-neighbour bands on binary salt
membership. The other three geological recipes retain zero-padded
morphology at the cube faces. All four use stabilized band denominators
and require both sides to be nonempty.
Appendix~\ref{sec:geo-boundaries} specifies the reductions. The final
comparison therefore evaluates complete model recipes, including these
choices, rather than varying architecture alone.

Signed-distance supervision uses one salt field in voxel units, clipped
at eight voxels and normalized by eight. Depth supervision uses the
source-array geometry: its plane normal is source axis 0 projected
orthogonally to the salt mask's first principal axis. If the projected
norm is below 0.1, the least-parallel source axis is substituted, with
axis-index tie breaking. Depth strata are two voxels wide, the region
margin is eight voxels, and sparse strata use a threshold of 64 voxels.
These widths match those used for ADNI and, in voxel units, for MSD's
$1\,\mathrm{mm}$ grid. Their numerical agreement supports comparison of
the constraint settings without assuming equal physical scales across
applications.
UNETR's coordinate mapping is restored after the horizontal transformations before
constructing depth weights. This is an annotation-derived coordinate;
it is not a measurement of geological age or a guaranteed vertical
stratigraphic ordering.

\subsubsection{Calibration, Scheduling and Compute Allowance}

The geological cap follows the same design principle as the medical
comparison: start from a short, practically useful update allowance for
nnU-Net, the most computationally expensive of the four implementations
per update, and give every architecture the corresponding FLOP allowance.
This choice aims to allow all models to learn within a restricted budget
despite their different computational costs. Models with cheaper updates
can consequently make more passes through the training subset. The
reference is update cost rather than parameter count; these two quantities
do not rank the architectures in the same order.

For the $96\times96\times96$ geological inputs, the reference allowance
was 152 nnU-Net updates, corresponding to eight complete passes through
19 training scenes. Multiplying this count by the original Dice-only
nnU-Net profile of $1{,}198.540306205$ GFLOPs per update established the
numerical cap. The final comparison retains that cap and charges both
training and calibration computation within it:

% SEISMIC_CAP from 152 times the original Dice-only nnU-Net update profile.
% The final native Dice+CE recipe retains that numerical cap.
\begin{equation}
 C_{\mathrm{train}}+C_{\mathrm{cal}}
 \leq 182{,}178.12654316\;\mathrm{GFLOPs}.
\label{eq:geological-compute-cap}
\end{equation}
This differs both in magnitude and accounting from the medical
optimization-only cap. Evaluation, diagnostic preflight and recipe
development remain outside this allowance. It is therefore not an
end-to-end compute budget, and absolute medical/geological scores do
not represent equal-compute comparisons across datasets.

Calibration uses all 19 training scenes at the frozen anchor, without
augmentation or validation inputs. The sequential gradient-ratio
procedure uses target/p95 limits of 3.6/4.5 for nnU-Net, 0.4/2.0 for
UNETR and 0.1/0.5 for SwinUNETR and the compact U-Net. The nnU-Net
and UNETR strengths were selected following exploratory comparisons;
they are not shared coefficients across architectures. For combinations,
sequential calibration can also change a term's coefficient when another
term is added. The comparisons therefore evaluate complete calibrated
configurations.

The unconstrained prefix and auxiliary ramp each last nine updates
for nnU-Net and 50 for the other models. SDF uses the early shared
ramp for SwinUNETR and UNETR in $S$, $B+S$ and $B+E+S$, and for
the compact U-Net in $B+S$. All other SDF configurations use the
frozen delayed policy, whose onset is placed near five ninths of the
nominal cap on the training trajectory, with a separate ramp of the
same length. The source schedule determines the exact integer update.
Learning-rate horizons stay fixed rather than being compressed to the
last affordable update.

The final native Dice-plus-cross-entropy nnU-Net recipe has a slightly
higher measured update cost than the original Dice-only profile. It
therefore fits 151 baseline updates within the retained cap: seven
completed epochs and 18 updates into the next, rather than the eight
complete epochs used to define the reference allowance. Reserving
calibration and charging auxiliary arithmetic further reduces the
training updates available to constrained configurations.
Table~\ref{tab:cap-training-exposure} reports the actual update counts,
completed epochs and remaining updates for every architecture under
both dataset-specific caps. Equal FLOPs specify the resource constraint;
the architectures can still be at different stages of learning.
The final admissible checkpoint supplies the reported score;
validation-best checkpoints and intermediate screening scores do not
replace it. Results appear in Section~\ref{sec:geo-results}.

\subsection{MSD: Medical Segmentation Decathlon}
\label{sec:msd-data-methods}

\subsubsection{Inputs and Frozen Partitions}

The MSD collection contains 263 labelled MRI crops with background,
anterior and posterior hippocampus classes \cite{simpson2019dataset}.

For the capped experiments, each image is z-score normalized over nonzero
intensities, then padded and centrally cropped with its labels to $64^3$.
This preserves voxel scale rather than resizing the anatomy. Validation
uses this prepared grid. Each of folds 0, 1 and 2 supplies ten support
crops and 52 validation crops, shared across models and objectives.
Seeds are 0, 2 and 20. Validation identifiers are disjoint across the
three folds, but the available crop identifiers do not establish
participant-level independence. MedSAM3 uses its separate native-grid
preparation and manifests
in Section~\ref{sec:medsam-methods}.

\subsubsection{Testing Proposed Anatomical Rules}
\label{sec:annotation-compatibility}

The MSD data descriptor \cite{simpson2019dataset} cites the manual
annotation protocols of Pruessner et al.\ \cite{pruessner2000volumetry}
and Woolard and Heckers \cite{woolard2012asymmetry}.
It identifies the last hippocampal-head slice as the coronal slice
containing the uncal apex; body and tail form the posterior label.
Turning this description
into an automatic constraint requires a detectable landmark, a known
orientation and a relation that remains meaningful on the stored grid.
The annotation audit tests these operational assumptions before imposing
them during learning.

Two analyses address the proposed A/P transition rules. A planar analysis tests
whether one coronal separation reproduces the stored labels in all
260 crops. Coronal-profile and image-cue analyses examine 52 original
validation cases for a reliable way to locate the transition.

The image-cue investigation includes automated and visual searches but
has no independently validated expert localization of the uncal apex.
The audit can therefore establish whether a tested numerical proxy fits
the available labels. It cannot determine whether the original annotators
misapplied the anatomical protocol. Section~\ref{sec:annotation-results}
reports the findings within the limitations and explains their
consequences for constraint design.



\subsection{ADNI: Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative}
\label{sec:adni-methods}

\subsubsection{Source Images and Reference Labels}

The ADNI dataset used here combines the final released hippocampal labels
and oriented MINC images from the EADC--ADNI harmonized-protocol resource
\cite{harpReleasedLabels}. Image and label pairs are identified through
subject and image metadata. Because the release notes a possible
alignment issue in converted NIfTI images, the builder uses the
original MINC arrays to export paired crops.

The preparation contains 135 subject identifiers and 135 image
identifiers, each contributing left and right hippocampal crops.
The resulting 270 volumes have size $32\times48\times40$ and binary
labels: zero for background and one for hippocampus. Laterality
identifies the crop; it does not define a further segmentation class.
Figure~\ref{fig:adni-data-examples} shows paired left and right crops,
their binary reference masks and annotation overlays. Both sides use
the same foreground class, without the anterior/posterior subdivision
of MSD.

\subsubsection{Preparation, Partitions and Spatial Geometry}

Crops are centred on the reference-mask bounding box, with identical
indices applied to the image. Their evaluation therefore assumes prior
hippocampal localization. The capped experiments use three partitions,
identified as folds 0, 1 and 2. Each partition groups both sides of a
subject together, with an eligible training pool of 108 subjects
(216 crops) and 27 validation subjects (54 crops), with no within-fold
subject overlap. Five selected training subjects contribute both sides:
ten support crops per fold, shared across models and seeds 0, 1 and 4.
Images are
z-score normalized over nonzero intensities and padded to $64^3$,
without resizing. MedSAM3 uses the separate
two-crop support protocol in Section~\ref{sec:medsam-adni-methods}.

The exported ADNI crops do not retain the original spacing and
orientation metadata, so spatial constraints are defined in voxel
coordinates. They retain the same geometric construction used for MSD:
signed distances are capped at eight voxels, and depth supervision uses
layers two voxels thick within the hippocampus and its eight-voxel
surrounding region. The depth plane passes through the hippocampus
centre and contains its main axis; in ADNI, an image-grid axis fixes
its orientation. Because MSD has verified $1\,\mathrm{mm}$ isotropic
spacing, its $2\,\mathrm{mm}$ layers and $8\,\mathrm{mm}$ surrounding
region match these widths numerically on the voxel grid. The two
formulations are therefore comparable in construction and grid-scale
settings, without assuming identical physical spacing or anatomical
orientation across the datasets.

\subsubsection{Models and Baseline Objectives}

from random initialization, use batch size one and predict the same
dataset-specific $64^3$ grid: three classes on MSD and two on ADNI. Their configurations instantiate the model

\subsubsection{Calibration and Activation}
\label{sec:calibration-methods}

Loss magnitudes depend on their supports and reductions. Calibration
therefore matches measured gradient scales before assigning fixed
coefficients. It uses the ten support cases after nine
baseline updates for nnU-Net and 50 for the other models. The network
is evaluated without changing its parameters, buffers or random-generator
states. For detached float32 primary logits $z_i$ with $m_i$ entries,
define
\begin{equation}
g_{i,0}=\nabla_{z_i}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{seg},i},
\qquad
g_{i,j}=\nabla_{z_i}\mathcal{L}_{j,i},
\qquad
\operatorname{RMS}(g)=
\sqrt{\frac{1}{m_i}\sum_{k=1}^{m_i}g_k^2}.
\label{eq:calibration-logit-gradients}
\end{equation}
nnU-Net's baseline derivative includes the primary output's native
deep-supervision weight; detached coarser outputs make no contribution
to this derivative.

Families are calibrated in the order $B\to E\to S\to D$, omitting
absent terms. If $K_j$ contains the families already calibrated, the
reference includes their weighted gradients:
\begin{equation}
g_{i,<j}=g_{i,0}+\sum_{k\in K_j}\lambda_k g_{i,k},
\qquad
r_{i,j}=\frac{\operatorname{RMS}(g_{i,j})}
                  {\operatorname{RMS}(g_{i,<j})}.
\label{eq:sequential-calibration-ratio}
\end{equation}
The coefficient is then
\begin{equation}
\lambda_j=
\min\left\{
\frac{\eta_a}{\operatorname{median}_i r_{i,j}},\,
\frac{\kappa_a}{Q_{0.95}(\{r_{i,j}\}_i)}
\right\}.
\label{eq:constraint-calibrated-coefficient}
\end{equation}
For nnU-Net, $(\eta_a,\kappa_a)=(0.6,0.75)$ in both datasets;
for the other three models, $(\eta_a,\kappa_a)=(0.1,0.5)$. The explicit
numeric protocol fields and saved calibration determine these settings.
The first bound targets the median gradient ratio and the second
limits its 95th percentile on the calibration cases. Adding gradient vectors
before taking their RMS retains interactions between their directions,
making the coefficient specific to the combination. Depth is calibrated
against preceding terms at full coefficients even when SDF is activated
later. These measurements use no validation labels and do not guarantee
the same gradient ratios throughout optimization.

Early terms follow
\begin{equation}
s_a^{\mathrm{early}}(t)=
\begin{cases}
0, & t\leq d_a,\\
\min\{1,(t-d_a)/w_a\}, & t>d_a.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:constraint-early-ramp}
\end{equation}
with baseline prefix and ramp length $d_a=w_a=9$ for nnU-Net and
$d_a=w_a=50$ otherwise. SDF either shares this schedule or starts an
analogous ramp after a delayed boundary near five ninths of the
optimization allowance.

\subsubsection{Resource Accounting and Evaluation}

The medical budget was anchored to nnU-Net because its measured training
update was the most expensive among the four implementations. Exploratory
learning curves informed the choice of 90 nnU-Net updates as the minimum
practical reference allowance for this short-budget comparison. With ten
training crops and one crop per update, this represents nine complete
passes through the selected training subset. The intention was to leave
all architectures a useful learning window while keeping computation
limited. This choice concerns computational cost, rather than model size
alone: UNETR has more parameters than nnU-Net, but cheaper updates in
these configurations.

The measured native nnU-Net baseline update costs
$355{,}293{,}896{,}301$ FLOPs. Multiplying it by the 90-update reference
schedule gives the common optimization ceiling used on both MSD and ADNI:
% CAP PROVENANCE: reports/four_models_six_optimization_31976_20261006/
% prepared_plan.json, budget_origin: 90 times measured native nnU-Net
% baseline update cost, selected following exploratory learning curves.
\begin{equation}
 C_{\max}=31{,}976{,}450{,}667{,}090\ \mathrm{FLOPs}
          =31{,}976.45066709\ \mathrm{GFLOPs}.
\label{eq:main-numerical-cap}
\end{equation}
Each other architecture receives this same FLOP allowance, with its
number of updates determined by its own measured cost. Cheaper models
therefore receive more updates and complete more epochs. The cap matches
computational allowance across architectures; it does not require them
to reach the same stage of learning or convergence.

The arithmetic ledger charges forward computation, active losses,
backward computation and the optimizer update, counting two FLOPs per
multiply--accumulate. An update is admitted only if its charge fits
within the remaining allowance. This defines the optimization resource
being compared; it does not measure GPU utilization or elapsed time.

Constraint calculations in the optimization graph are included.
Construction of fixed reference targets, validation,
loading and storage are excluded. Calibration is accounted separately.
Thus, the matched quantity is optimization computation, while total
training-plus-calibration work can differ between objectives.

Each dataset contributes $4\times14\times3\times3=504$ runs.
The primary aggregate includes every retained fold/seed cell, giving
three seeds in each fold. The cap is the same numerical allowance on
both datasets, but their metrics concern different label vocabularies
and their starting difficulty need not be equal. Calibration remains
additional work outside this allowance.

Table~\ref{tab:cap-training-exposure} translates the admitted updates
into completed epochs. Here an epoch is one complete pass through the
selected training subset: ten crops for MSD and ADNI, or 19 scenes for
GeoFWI3D. Training visits each example once in a shuffled order before
starting the next pass, with batch size one. For $U$ training updates
and $N$ selected examples, the number of completed epochs and the
updates in the unfinished next epoch are
\begin{equation}
 E=\left\lfloor\frac{U}{N}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad R=U-NE,\qquad 0\leq R<N.
\label{eq:completed-training-epochs}
\end{equation}
Thus, 89 medical training updates are reported as eight completed
epochs plus nine further updates. Calibration evaluations do not count
as training epochs. These counts describe the training allowed by the
FLOP cap; an epoch limit is not imposed in addition to it.

\subsection{SAM3 Adaptation on GeoFWI3D}
\label{sec:sam3-methods}

\subsubsection{Initialization, Support Data and Predictions}

The geological adaptation study starts from the general SAM~3 checkpoint
\cite{carion2025sam3}, with fresh LoRA factors whose initial product is
zero. Only the 18,497,664 adapter parameters are optimized; the base
weights remain frozen. No medical adapter is loaded. The fixed query is
\emph{salt body}. Its foreground response combines mask, query and
presence confidences as in the binary projection of
Section~\ref{sec:medsam-adni-methods}; the complementary response represents
background. Argmax produces mutually exclusive salt/background labels.
Whole-volume predictions are reconstructed from slices and the fixed
query, without reference-mask localization prompts.

The design crosses folds 0, 1 and 3 with seeds 0, 1 and 2 and the fourteen
objectives in Table~\ref{tab:constraint-combinations}, giving 126 runs.
Each fold uses five labelled support volumes, selected as the first five
of its established geological support list, and 96 held-out volumes.
Supports remain fixed across seeds and objectives. The native
$96\times96\times96$ volumes are sampled as adjacent-slice pairs;
reflectivity is mapped from $[-1,1]$ to $[0,1]$ before conversion to the
model's image input.

\subsubsection{Objectives, Calibration and Update Schedule}

The SAM3 baseline uses a composite training objective: detection
classification and bounding-box losses, a presence loss, and focal and
Dice mask losses. The Dice mask loss is a differentiable penalty on soft
predictions and is only one component of the quantity minimized during
training. In the tables, \emph{Baseline} names this complete objective;
\emph{Baseline} $+B$, for example, adds the band penalty to it. The
reported Dice scores are calculated separately from the final discrete
segmentations, as described below.

Constrained variants retain the complete baseline objective and add the
selected spatial terms. Bands and edges supervise the salt
mask on regions and neighbouring voxel pairs defined from the full
three-dimensional annotation. Sampled losses use full-volume counts
and sampling corrections to preserve their volumetric weighting. The two-step,
zero-padded construction retains crop-face support. Signed distances
use unit-grid distances clipped at eight voxels and normalized by eight.
Depth weights use source-array geometry, without an anatomical
orientation assumption. The sampled reductions are given in
Appendix~\ref{sec:sampled-pretrained-supervision}.

Every arm receives 25 optimizer updates, each accumulating two
adjacent-slice pairs: 100 slice and single-class-query presentations in
total, drawn repeatedly from the five volumes. AdamW uses weight decay
0.01 and gradient-norm clipping at 1. The learning-rate schedule retains
its 200-update horizon, 20-update warmup and peak/final rates of
$5\times10^{-5}$ and $5\times10^{-6}$. Training uses the first 25 updates
of that schedule; the decay is not compressed to finish at update 25.

Calibration uses four anchor pairs per support volume, for twenty
probes. Bands are calibrated at initialization for each fold/seed cell.
Other selected terms are calibrated sequentially after five prefix
updates, in the order bands, edges, signed distance and depth, retaining
the already calibrated band coefficient when present. Each additional
term targets a median gradient ratio of 0.1 with a 95th-percentile cap
of 0.5; coefficients then remain fixed. Terms awaiting calibration have
zero weight during the prefix. The constraint ramp follows the global
20-update warmup, so a newly calibrated term enters at update 6 with
ramp factor $6/20$.

\subsubsection{Paired Evaluation and Resource Accounting}

Each arm restores the same base and initial adapters and uses the matched
fold/seed sampling schedule. After update 25, the final adapter produces
whole-volume segmentations for all five support and 96 held-out volumes.
\emph{Validation Dice} measures hard salt overlap on the held-out
volumes; \emph{training-set Dice} measures the same overlap on the five
support volumes used for fitting. Neither score includes any of the
other training-loss components. Dice and foreground FN/FP follow the
reductions in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-methods}. Paired Dice gains
use only the SAM3 baseline from the same fold and seed;
historical pilots, MedSAM3 controls and conventional geological
baselines do not enter these differences.

The completed inventory contains 76 runs on Colab A100-SXM4-80GB
GPUs and 50 on A100 80GB PCIe GPUs allocated as 4g.40gb MIG instances.
All runs retain the same scientific source identity. Archived hashes
verify unchanged base parameters and matching initial adapters, support
arrays and sampling schedules within every fold/seed comparison.
Hardware allocation was not a fully crossed factor; timing comparisons
must retain their execution platform and measurement scope.
Resource records also distinguish recovery attempts from the final
25-update trajectory. The 25-update allowance
measures task adaptation and excludes pretraining. It is distinct from the geological FLOP cap
and the 200-update medical adaptation schedule.

\subsection{MedSAM3 Adaptation on MSD}
\label{sec:medsam-methods}

\subsubsection{Initialization, Supports and Prediction}

Every arm starts from the same SAM3 base checkpoint and released medical
LoRA adapter \cite{carion2025sam3,liu2025medsam3}. Optimization updates
916 adapter tensors containing 18,497,664 parameters while retaining
the frozen base weights. The baseline retains the composite SAM3
training objective described in Section~\ref{sec:sam3-methods}, including
its focal and soft Dice mask losses. Each constrained variant adds its
spatial penalties to this objective. These losses determine the adapter
updates; the Dice scores reported after fitting are separate overlap
measurements. The ADNI adaptation in
Section~\ref{sec:medsam-adni-methods} shares this initialization and
optimizer protocol; its dataset-specific choices are described there.

MSD uses folds 0, 1 and 3 with seeds 17, 83 and 191. Each fold has five
support crops and respectively 46, 52 and 44 evaluation crops. The 15
support crops and 142 evaluation crops are distinct and do not overlap.
The campaign evaluates the baseline and thirteen constrained variants,
giving $14\times3\times3=126$ planned objective runs. Each variant
retains the same five-volume support set and 200-update schedule.
Paired gains use the baseline recorded with the corresponding runs;
repeated controls are not pooled or counted as additional objectives.
Execution and calibration records are indexed in
Appendix~\ref{sec:evidence-register}. Fold selection followed earlier
exploratory outcomes, and participant identities are unavailable.

MSD images and masks use the closest canonical NIfTI orientation.
Image intensities are scaled
between the first and 99th percentiles, clipped to $[0,1]$ and converted
to eight-bit planes for the processor. The fixed queries are
\emph{anterior hippocampus} and \emph{posterior hippocampus}.
Held-out masks supply no prompts.
Mask, query and presence confidences define foreground responses;
their log-odds and a zero background logit are normalized by softmax.
Argmax assigns a mutually exclusive class, and the native-grid plane
predictions are assembled into a volume.

\subsubsection{Spatial Objectives and Optimization}

Bands and edges average the separate background, anterior and
posterior masks, using three-dimensional reference supports and
full-volume counts while sampling image planes. SDF supervises the
foreground classes; depth weights the true-class cross-entropy.
The half-voxel signed field is clipped at eight units and divided by
eight, using MSD's verified 1 mm grid. Depth uses physical geometry.

In both medical adaptation studies, support volumes and anchor planes
are sampled uniformly. Bands, SDF
and depth use the anchor; edges additionally use the adjacent plane.
Full-volume denominators and the appropriate sampling factors preserve
the corresponding volumetric reductions in expectation
(Appendix~\ref{sec:technical-details}).

Every arm receives 200 optimizer updates with two adjacent-slice pairs
per update. This gives 800 slice presentations from the fixed support
set, or 1,600 foreground-query presentations on MSD.
These repetitions do not increase the annotation count. AdamW uses
20 warmup updates, learning rates from $5\times10^{-5}$ to
$5\times10^{-6}$, weight decay 0.01 and gradient-norm clipping at one.
All selected auxiliary terms ramp as $\min\{1,t/20\}$ from the first
update, without the delayed SDF policy of the conventional models.
Recorded schedule hashes verify matched sampling across arms.

Calibration uses four anchors per support volume, giving twenty slabs
on MSD. Gradients are measured with respect to trainable
LoRA parameters, retaining vector interactions in the sequential order
$B\to E\to S\to D$. Equation~\ref{eq:constraint-calibrated-coefficient}
uses $(\eta_a,\kappa_a)=(0.1,0.5)$ for both adaptation studies. Coefficients
are specific to the selected combination and are computed from support
data only. Within each fold, coefficients are shared across optimization
seeds according to the saved calibration-source records. The $B$ and
$B+E$ variants retain their recorded band and edge calibration; the
other eleven constrained variants use the separately audited calibration
associated with their execution batch. Coefficients are not substituted
between these groups.

\subsubsection{Evaluation and Execution Measurements}

In both medical studies, evaluation uses the final 200-update adapter,
without early stopping or validation-dependent checkpoint selection.
For MSD, hard Dice is calculated separately for the anterior and
posterior labels in each reconstructed volume, then the two scores are
averaged. The primary score excludes background. Validation Dice applies
this calculation to held-out crops that did not contribute to
fine-tuning; training-set Dice applies it to the five support crops.
Both columns report segmentation overlap, not the composite loss or its
Dice-loss component. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-methods} gives the
formula and the complementary background and all-class measures.

Per-volume Dice is averaged within a run, then across seeds within a
fold and equally across folds. The same hierarchy applies to mean
per-crop confusion counts. This retains equal fold weights despite
different MSD validation-set sizes.

The update budget describes additional task adaptation, excluding
pretraining. It is not a FLOP match with the conventional studies.
Runtime records identify the execution environment and measured scope;
training-loop timers alone do not include initialization, calibration,
validation, inference or peak-memory use. Production CUDA execution
does not establish bitwise determinism from a common seed alone.

\subsection{MedSAM3 Adaptation on ADNI}
\label{sec:medsam-adni-methods}

\subsubsection{Supports, Preprocessing and Prediction}

ADNI uses folds 0, 1 and 3 and seeds 0, 1 and 2, with fourteen objectives
and 126 runs. Each fold has two labelled support crops from two subjects
and 54 held-out crops from 27 other subjects. Support selection follows
the saved subject manifest and alternates left/right crops. Folds 0 and
3 share their two support crops; fold 1 shares one of them and changes
the other. Across these folds there are only three distinct support
crops. The fold comparisons therefore vary the evaluation partition
without providing three independent draws of a two-crop training set.

The study shares the frozen SAM~3 base, released medical LoRA
initialization and composite baseline training objective
described in Section~\ref{sec:medsam-methods}.
ADNI retains the stored array orientation. Intensities undergo the
same first-to-99th-percentile scaling, clipping and eight-bit conversion
as MSD. A single fixed query, \emph{hippocampus}, supplies the foreground
response for the binary semantic projection; held-out masks supply no
prompts. Native-grid plane predictions are assembled into a volume.

\subsubsection{Spatial Objectives, Optimization and Evaluation}

Bands and edges act on the single hippocampus mask and its complement,
without a second background-mask average. They retain three-dimensional
reference supports and full-volume counts during plane sampling.
SDF supervises the hippocampus class, with the half-voxel field clipped
at eight unit array voxels and divided by eight. Depth weights the
true-class cross-entropy using the source-axis geometric reference,
without an anatomical orientation claim.

The uniform support and anchor sampling, adjacent-plane edge evaluation,
AdamW settings and auxiliary activation schedule follow
Section~\ref{sec:medsam-methods}. Each arm receives 200 updates with two
adjacent-slice pairs per update: 800 slice presentations and, with one
foreground query, 800 foreground-query presentations. Calibration uses
four anchors per support volume, giving eight slabs. It uses the same
sequential gradient calibration and $(\eta_a,\kappa_a)=(0.1,0.5)$,
with coefficients specific to each selected combination and computed
from support data only. The archives retain the calibration used by
every run, and schedule hashes verify matched sampling across arms.

Evaluation uses the final 200-update adapter without early stopping or
validation-dependent checkpoint selection. The reported score is hard
hippocampus Dice, excluding background: validation Dice uses the 54
held-out crops, while training-set Dice uses the two support crops.
As in MSD, these are overlap scores computed after fitting, separate
from every component of the training objective. Per-volume scores and
confusion counts are averaged within runs, across seeds within each
fold and equally across folds. Execution measurements retain the scope
defined in Section~\ref{sec:medsam-methods}; the update allowance excludes
pretraining and does not match the conventional FLOP budget.

\subsection{Evaluation Measures}
\label{sec:evaluation-methods}

Dice serves two distinct roles. During training, a differentiable soft
Dice loss contributes to the architecture-specific optimization
objective. During evaluation, hard Dice measures overlap between the
final discrete predictions and reference labels. Every Dice score in
the results tables uses this second definition, including scores on the
training support set. Validation Dice uses held-out examples;
training-set Dice uses examples supplied during fitting. Neither score
contains focal, cross-entropy, box, presence or spatial-constraint loss
terms, and neither is the value of the Dice loss used during training.

Voxelwise argmax defines the predicted label. For reference region
$Y_{i,c}$ and prediction $\widehat Y_{i,c}$, hard class Dice is
\begin{equation}
 D_{i,c}=\frac{2|Y_{i,c}\cap\widehat Y_{i,c}|}
 {|Y_{i,c}|+|\widehat Y_{i,c}|}.
\label{eq:reported-hard-dice}
\end{equation}
A present reference class with no predicted voxels scores zero.
The MSD volume score is the mean anterior/posterior Dice,
$D_i=(D_{i,A}+D_{i,P})/2$, and the ADNI volume score is hippocampus Dice. Geological evaluation
uses salt Dice.
Background is excluded from these primary metrics. Volumes receive
equal weight in a run mean. Section~\ref{sec:background-dice-results}
adds background and all-class Dice as complementary measures on
the same predictions, retaining this order of averaging.

For objective $j$, model $m$, fold $f$ and seed $s$, the paired change is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{m,j,f,s}=100\left(
 \overline D_{m,j,f,s}-\overline D_{m,\mathrm{base},f,s}\right).
\label{eq:paired-reported-gain}
\end{equation}
Dice is reported on a percentage scale and differences in percentage
points. Reported standard deviations are sample standard deviations across
the three seed-averaged fold means; paired-gain dispersions use the three
fold-mean differences. Positive-change counts use all nine matched
fold/seed cells. Each table identifies its reduction explicitly. They are not
confidence intervals over independent datasets: seeds share data,
training pools overlap and configuration selection is exploratory.
Dispersion across cases is identified separately from dispersion
across fold/seed cells.

Pretrained-model confusion matrices use reference classes as rows and
predicted classes as columns. For MSD, four disjoint errors are counted: background
predicted as foreground, foreground predicted as background, anterior
predicted as posterior, and posterior predicted as anterior. Their
sum gives all incorrectly labelled voxels. Conventional-run error counts pool the full validation grid before
averaging runs. MedSAM3 tables average errors per crop within each run
and then weight folds equally. All counts cover the complete prediction,
including voxels outside the boundary bands. SAM3 FN/FP are likewise
averaged per held-out volume within a run, then over seeds and folds;
each run contains 96 validation volumes. Binary ADNI and GeoFWI3D have
no A/P swaps. The different error-count reductions are labelled and are not compared
as if they had the same denominator.

\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

The six-study design covers 1,890 completed objective runs: 630 on
GeoFWI3D and 1,260 across the medical comparisons and adaptations.
The conventional geological results are presented first, followed by
MSD and ADNI. The adaptation results then follow the order SAM3 on
GeoFWI3D, MedSAM3 on MSD and MedSAM3 on ADNI. A complementary analysis compares
foreground, background and all-class Dice before the MSD annotation
audit. All SAM3 tables use the complete 126-run inventory, including
the error and complementary Dice analyses. Every objective is evaluated
against a matched final-budget baseline.
Tables label training variants as Baseline or Baseline plus the added
constraints, and report evaluation Dice in percent and paired changes
in percentage points. The symbols $B,E,S,D$ denote bands, edges,
signed distance and depth supervision. A training-set or support Dice
column reports segmentation overlap on the labelled training examples,
not the value of the training objective.
Unless otherwise stated, $\pm$ gives the sample standard deviation of
the three seed-averaged fold means. It describes observed variation,
not a confidence interval. Highest-mean objectives are selected after
inspection, so their apparent advantage also reflects selection among
the tested configurations.

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Error Analysis}
\label{sec:error-analysis}
\label{sec:capped-errors-resources}

The following tables report errors for every training variant in the
six studies, in the same order as the Dice comparisons. A false negative
(FN) is reference foreground predicted as background; a false positive
(FP) is reference background predicted as foreground. On MSD, A/P swaps
count anterior voxels predicted as posterior plus posterior voxels
predicted as anterior. These swaps preserve foreground membership and
are counted separately from FN and FP. They are not applicable
to binary ADNI or GeoFWI3D segmentation. All counts cover the complete
validation prediction, including locations outside the constraint bands.

For the conventional models, counts are pooled over the validation
volumes in each run. For SAM3 and MedSAM3, counts are averaged per volume
or crop within a run. Each complete condition then averages the three
seeds within each fold and weights the three folds equally. Tables give
the resulting mean and sample standard deviation across the three fold
means. Both the mean and its standard deviation use the power of ten
shown in the column heading, with displayed values rounded to two decimal
places. For example, $2.12\pm0.70$ under $\times10^6$ denotes
approximately $2.12\times10^6\pm0.70\times10^6$ voxels. Full-precision
counts are retained in the numerical archive.
These dispersions describe variation between the evaluated folds;
they are not confidence intervals. Counts retain each study's evaluation
grid and denominator, so their absolute magnitudes should not be compared
across studies as a measure of relative performance.

Within each model and dataset, variants are ordered from the lowest to
the highest mean total error: FN $+$ FP for binary segmentation, and
FN $+$ FP $+$ A/P swaps for MSD. These categories are disjoint, so their
sum counts all incorrectly labelled voxels. Ordering uses unrounded
means.

\subsubsection{GeoFWI3D: Conventional Models}

Table~\ref{tab:geo-all-errors} includes all 504 evaluations, including
the nine recovered baseline records. For each architecture's highest-mean
Dice variant, nnU-Net reduces both FN (34.4\%) and FP (11.4\%) relative
to baseline. SwinUNETR and 3D U-Net reduce FP by 12.4\% and 26.9\%,
respectively, with FN increases of 0.7\% and 3.9\%. UNETR's $B+E$
configuration instead reduces FN by 4.3\% while increasing FP by 2.9\%.
Thus, the overlap gains arise from different changes in foreground
retention and extension. The full table also retains variants with
negative Dice changes; no error category is selected for reporting
according to whether it improves.

\subsection{Foreground, Background and All-Class Dice}
\label{sec:background-dice-results}

The primary Dice scores emphasize the labelled target structures.
Evaluating background separately complements them by checking whether
foreground gains accompany improved agreement in the surrounding region.
The recovered per-volume confusion matrices permit both measurements
on the same saved predictions, without further training or inference.
Write $D_{i,\mathrm{fg}}$ for the primary volume score and
$D_{i,\mathrm{bg}}=D_{i,0}$ for background Dice. Equal weighting of
all classes gives
\begin{equation}
D_{i,\mathrm{all}}=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{2D_{i,\mathrm{fg}}+D_{i,\mathrm{bg}}}{3},
 & \text{MSD},\\[.6em]
\displaystyle\frac{D_{i,\mathrm{fg}}+D_{i,\mathrm{bg}}}{2},
 & \text{ADNI and GeoFWI3D}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:reported-all-class-dice}
\end{equation}
MSD retains separate anterior and posterior classes. The class scores
are calculated within each volume, followed by equal-volume, seed and
fold reductions as in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-methods}; confusion
counts are not pooled across volumes to form a substitute Dice score.

across both regions, while the foreground measure retains sensitivity
to the target structure.

The higher absolute all-class scores result from the added contribution
of background. Their gains are smaller because background changes
relatively little: its inclusion reduces the foreground weight to
two-thirds on MSD and one-half on the binary tasks. The selected
MSD SwinUNETR gain changes from 11.97 to 8.27 points; the ADNI

the same predictions under different class weights.

all 193 complete constrained-objective comparisons with recoverable
background scores, the same 179 mean effects are positive and the
same fourteen are negative under foreground and all-class Dice.
The highest-mean objective is unchanged in all fifteen model/dataset

counts: nine of nine in thirteen comparisons and eight of nine for
geological UNETR and SAM3. Bands alone still improve all 72 conventional medical
model/fold/seed cells under both measures.

Six of 1,737 individual paired differences change sign, all on
GeoFWI3D; their foreground magnitudes are below 0.14 points and
their all-class magnitudes below 0.03 points. Some secondary objective
rankings also change. These small differences leave the reported
mean benefits and negative findings intact, without establishing
statistical superiority between closely ranked objectives.

is included. In particular, the negative $B+E+S$ finding persists
under both measures. These checks use the complete campaign.

Together, the measures show that the selected foreground improvements
persist when background participates in the class average. They provide
a broader assessment of overlap while preserving the original
foreground endpoint. This is a sensitivity analysis of the metric on
the same experiments, so it retains their selection and replication
limitations and supplies no additional evidence of surface accuracy
or downstream usefulness.

\subsection{Scope and Limitations of the Evidence}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsubsection{Annotation Compatibility and the Anterior/Posterior Transition}
\label{sec:annotation-results}

The audit in Section~\ref{sec:annotation-compatibility} examines whether
the published anterior/posterior annotation convention can support an
additional anatomical constraint. It tests compatibility with the
released labels and the feasibility of locating the transition
automatically.

The MSD dataset description attributes manual tracing to two earlier
hippocampal annotation protocols \cite{simpson2019dataset}.
Pruessner et al.\ \cite{pruessner2000volumetry} describe manual volumetry
with simultaneous inspection of sagittal, coronal and horizontal views.
Woolard and Heckers \cite{woolard2012asymmetry} build on that protocol:
sagittal tracing is refined in coronal views, and the hippocampus is then
divided into anterior uncus and posterior body and tail. Their anatomical
cues include ``more than one cut through the hippocampus'' in a coronal
section and a medial protrusion towards the crus cerebri; the division
is checked in the sagittal view.

The MSD paper specifies the final head section as the ``coronal slice
containing the uncal apex'' \cite{simpson2019dataset}. These descriptions
motivate two candidate constraints: a single coronal A/P separation and
a transition located from the appearance or disappearance of the folded
uncal profile. The audit asks whether these descriptions yield
reproducible rules for the released crops. Exact separation on the stored
voxel grid and disconnected mask components are operational proxies;
the papers describe an anatomical tracing procedure using multiple
views, not these numerical tests.

The planar analysis finds that 58 of 260 crops cannot be reproduced
exactly by a single coronal A/P division. The best planes disagree
with 1,388 of 856,754 foreground voxels overall, approximately
0.162\%. A strict universal rule therefore has counterexamples,
although the aggregate discrepancy is small. This supports treating
the division as broadly plane-like while retaining its actual discrete
annotation for supervision.

% PROFILE AUDIT: experiments/uncal_validation_profiles_20260921/audit.json;
% metric_counts: split_min2_at_cut=2, split_min2_near_cut=2, n=52.
The profile analysis examines a numerical proxy for the multiple-cut
cue described by Woolard and Heckers \cite{woolard2012asymmetry}.
Only two of 52 validation crops contain two disconnected foreground
components of at least two voxels each in the final anterior section;
allowing a window of three sections on either side of the fitted
interface still identifies only two crops. This criterion therefore
does not recover the annotated transition consistently. A folded
three-dimensional structure can intersect one plane several times,
and distinct tissue profiles can remain connected in the discrete mask.
Nor does the absence of a visible separation prove the absence of the
anatomical landmark. The explored image cues did not establish a
universally reliable automatic transition location.

These findings guide the constraint design: the annotated A/P interface
provides the classwise supervision, while a strict plane or an automatic
uncal-profile rule is not imposed universally. This avoids penalizing
predictions that agree with the stored labels but violate a simplified
geometric proxy. The audit establishes the behaviour of those proxies;
without an expert landmark reference, it does not determine whether
the original annotators misapplied the anatomical protocol.

\subsubsection{Limitations of the Experimental Findings}
\label{sec:experimental-limitations}

The study evaluates spatial supervision across several architectures,
medical and geological datasets, and both training from scratch and
pretrained-model adaptation. Matched comparisons and repeated fold--seed
evaluations characterize its contribution under the specified experimental
conditions. The scope of these findings is defined by the datasets,
training budgets and objective configurations examined.

The medical experiments use prepared hippocampus crops, while the
geological experiments use synthetic volumes. Evaluation on additional
anatomical structures, uncropped images and measured seismic data would
establish how broadly the observed behaviour transfers. Similarly, the
computational limits are an intentional part of the experimental design:
the results describe performance within those limits, while persistence
of the gains under substantially longer training remains to be assessed.

The comparisons evaluate complete calibrated objectives. In particular,
depth supervision combines additional class supervision with spatial
weighting; a matched unweighted control would isolate the contribution
of the weighting itself.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

This thesis demonstrates that spatial information already present in
segmentation annotations can be used to improve predictions when labelled
examples and training computation are limited. Boundary membership,
local label transitions, classwise distances and depth-based supervision
provide additional learning signals from the same reference masks.
Their practical value is demonstrated by higher validation Dice under
the tested training budgets, without requiring additional manual
annotations. The contribution is therefore both a formulation of spatial
supervision and experimental evidence that it can make the available
labels and computation more effective.

The comparisons from scratch establish this benefit across four
architectures and three datasets. In every model--dataset setting, the
evaluated configurations include a spatially supervised objective with
higher mean validation Dice than the matched baseline. The evidence
covers anterior/posterior hippocampus segmentation on MSD, binary
hippocampus segmentation on ADNI and salt segmentation on GeoFWI3D.
The improvements therefore extend across convolutional and transformer
models, binary and multiclass tasks, and medical and geological images.
Charging the additional constraint computation within each study's
training allowance makes these gains evidence of better segmentation
at a fixed computational budget. For applications where annotation and
optimization are expensive, this is a concrete benefit: more accurate
segmentations can be obtained from the resources already allocated to
training.

Spatial supervision also improves the adaptation of pretrained models.
MedSAM3 benefits on both medical datasets with only five labelled support
crops on MSD and two on ADNI, within the same 200-update schedule as its
unconstrained baseline. SAM3 extends this result to geological images,
using five labelled volumes and 25 updates. These experiments show that
pretraining and task-specific spatial supervision can contribute
together: useful pretrained representations can be further specialized
through the geometry of a small support set. The gains are achieved by
updating low-rank adapters while keeping the base weights frozen, making
the approach relevant to adapting large models with limited additional
training.

The three-fold, three-seed comparisons show how these benefits vary with
the training conditions. The complementary Dice analysis also confirms
that the selected improvements persist when background participates in
the class average. This strengthens the conclusion that the methods
improve segmentation overlap beyond a single choice of foreground-only
reporting. The preferred combination nevertheless depends on the model
and task; adding every available term is not always the best choice.
The results support selecting and calibrating spatial supervision for
the intended setting, with the full comparisons and their limitations
reported in Chapter~\ref{sec:results}.

The common LTN/logLTN formulation makes this approach adaptable by
connecting each supervised property to a defined domain, predicate and
training loss. It distinguishes supervision of an object's exterior
from supervision of its internal classes and allows the geometry to be
specified for each dataset. The constraints act during training; the
resulting models retain their usual inference inputs and require no
reference-derived targets when segmenting a new volume. Annotation-derived
spatial supervision thus provides a practical way to improve both
models trained from scratch and adapted foundation models, while making
fuller use of limited labelled data and computational resources.

\subsection{Future Work}\label{sec:future-work}
\label{sec:pending-results}

The demonstrated gains provide a foundation for extending spatial
supervision to richer anatomical tasks, broader imaging populations and
more flexible adaptation strategies. A central direction is to combine
the general constraints developed here with knowledge specific to the
segmented structure. This would build on their ability to extract more
supervision from limited annotations while addressing properties that
membership, local transitions and distance weighting do not describe
individually.

Additional hippocampus datasets offer concrete opportunities for this
extension. The repository contains preparation code for MNI-HISUB25,
whose annotations distinguish CA1--3, the subicular complex and CA4--dentate
gyrus \cite{kulaga2015subfields}, and for the CoBrA hippocampal subfield
atlas, which provides a finer subdivision including CA1, CA2/3 and a
separate label for the strata radiatum, lacunosum and moleculare
\cite{winterburn2013atlas}. Neither resource is evaluated in this thesis.
They would extend the investigation from whole-hippocampus and A/P
segmentation to smaller internal structures. The HFH resource, containing
images of subjects with temporal lobe epilepsy and nonepileptic subjects,
offers a further extension to a different clinical population
\cite{jafari2011dataset}; the repository currently contains an inspection
helper for this resource. Comparisons could retain each dataset's native
subfield labels and assess transfer of whole-hippocampus supervision where
foreground definitions can be harmonized.

These datasets would support families of anatomically specific
constraints. Candidate statements could describe which subfields share
an interface, their relative positions within the hippocampus and the
continuity of thin annotated layers. Shape constraints could describe
cross-sectional thickness and its variation along an individual
hippocampal long axis, while connectivity terms could discourage isolated
predictions or interruptions within a structure. The relations would be
defined from the relevant annotation protocol and expert knowledge, with
soft tolerances that accommodate individual anatomy and pathological
variation. This would make it possible to test whether combining general
spatial supervision with structure-specific knowledge produces further
gains, particularly for small subregions with few annotated examples.

The annotation audit also motivates a more flexible anatomical transition
constraint. Expert landmark annotations and multiplanar image context
could support a confidence-weighted transition region around the uncal
apex. Such a formulation could represent uncertainty in the A/P interface
and follow the subject's anatomy rather than imposing a single fixed
cut. Comparing landmark-guided supervision with the existing classwise
terms would examine the additional value of this anatomical information.
The same principle could guide constraints on other internal boundaries
whose appearance varies across subjects or imaging protocols.

For pretrained models, a promising extension is to combine spatial
supervision with richer volumetric context and multiple image contrasts
where available. Experiments could vary adapter capacity, the layers
being adapted and the number of neighbouring slices processed together,
while measuring the accuracy obtained for the added computation. Varying
support-set size and composition would show how the benefits scale with
additional annotation. An active-learning study could then use predictive
uncertainty and anatomical inconsistency to select informative volumes
or regions for expert annotation, testing whether targeted selection
further reduces the amount of manual work needed for adaptation.

The weighting of constraints could also become more responsive to the
learning process. Training-based estimates of class difficulty or target
confidence could determine where and when each term contributes most.
Matched ablations of support geometry, normalization and scheduling,
including an unweighted classification control for depth supervision,
would identify which ingredients account for the gains and help simplify
future recipes. Evaluating several support sizes and compute allowances
would map where each objective is most useful, with matched data exposure
as a complementary comparison. Independent studies with choices fixed in
advance and evaluation on new participants would reinforce the evidence
for these selected procedures across populations and training budgets.

\section{Numerical and Implementation Details}
\label{sec:technical-details}

The main constraint chapter defines the supervised properties and their
losses. This appendix supplies the stable evaluations, derivatives and
protocol-specific reductions needed to reproduce them.

\subsection{Stable Membership Evaluation}

For a class group $F$ and its complement $\overline F$, define
$\operatorname{LSE}(z_U)=\log\sum_{c\in U}\exp z_c$. The grouped logit
\begin{equation}
s_F=\operatorname{LSE}(z_F)-\operatorname{LSE}(z_{\overline F})
\label{eq:grouped-predicate-logit}
\end{equation}
satisfies $\sigma(s_F)=\sum_{c\in F}p_c$. On MSD, $F=\{A,P\}$
and the complement contains only background. The stable membership loss is
\begin{equation}
e_i(v)=h_i(v)\operatorname{softplus}(-s_i(v))
       +(1-h_i(v))\operatorname{softplus}(s_i(v)).
\label{eq:band-stable-bce}
\end{equation}
Grouped logits and the fused loss use float32; classwise log-probabilities
use log-softmax. These evaluations avoid taking logarithms of rounded
probabilities.

Adding $\varepsilon$ to a nonempty support count multiplies its exact
mean by $\rho=|U|/(|U|+\varepsilon)$. Thus, for band-side means
$b_i^s=-\ell_\theta(\Phi_i^s)$ and
$\rho_i^s=n_i^s/(n_i^s+\varepsilon)$,
$\mathcal L_{B,i}=\tfrac12\sum_s\rho_i^s b_i^s$ and
\begin{equation}
\exp(-\mathcal{L}_{B,i})
=\mathcal{G}_\theta(\Phi_i^{\mathrm{in}})^{\rho_i^{\mathrm{in}}/2}
 \mathcal{G}_\theta(\Phi_i^{\mathrm{out}})^{\rho_i^{\mathrm{out}}/2}.
\label{eq:balanced-band-satisfaction}
\end{equation}
The count factors are retained in both values and gradients. Empty
supports follow the validity rules of the corresponding protocol.

\subsection{Relational and Signed-Distance Derivatives}

Band and depth losses have the usual weighted cross-entropy derivatives.
For edges, let $\mathcal N_i^B(u)$ contain the neighbours of $u$ in
the selected pair graph. The case-loss derivatives are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{E,i}}{\partial q_i(u)}
 &=\frac{2}{n_i^E}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{N}_i^B(u)}
       \bigl(\delta_i(u)-\delta_i(v)\bigr),\\
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{E,i}}{\partial s_i(u)}
 &=q_i(u)\bigl(1-q_i(u)\bigr)
   \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{E,i}}{\partial q_i(u)}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:edge-logit-gradient}
\end{equation}
The first expression applies the graph Laplacian to the membership
residual; the sigmoid factor attenuates the logit gradient near saturation.

For the MSD signed-distance term, define $\bar\varphi_{i,0}=0$ and
$\bar\varphi_{i,c}=\varphi_{i,c}$ for $c\in\{A,P\}$. Then
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SDF},i}}
     {\partial z_{\theta,i,k}(v)}
=\frac{p_{i,k}(v)}{2|\Omega_i|}
 \left[\bar\varphi_{i,k}(v)
       -\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}p_{i,c}(v)\bar\varphi_{i,c}(v)\right].
\label{eq:sdf-logit-gradient}
\end{equation}
The annotation-dependent offset that makes the loss nonnegative does
not change its derivative. Batch reduction, calibrated coefficients and
activation schedules multiply these case derivatives as specified in
Equation~\ref{eq:scheduled-constraint-objective}.

\subsection{Physical Geometry of the Depth Construction}

For the MSD hippocampal union $M_i$, the physical centroid and covariance are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mu_i&=\frac{1}{|M_i|}\sum_{v\in M_i}r_i(v),\\
\Sigma_i&=\frac{1}{|M_i|}\sum_{v\in M_i}
       (r_i(v)-\mu_i)(r_i(v)-\mu_i)^{\mathsf T}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:depth-centroid-covariance}
\end{equation}
The dominant unit eigenvector is oriented anteriorly to choose its
sign. Reversing this sign leaves the projected rightward normal in
Equation~\ref{eq:depth-plane-normal} unchanged. The principal-axis
eigenvalue gap is recorded as a diagnostic, without replacing
ambiguous geometry by a zero constraint.

The surrounding region is
\begin{equation}
R_i=\left\{v\in\Omega_i:
       \min_{u\in M_i}\|r_i(v)-r_i(u)\|_2\leq m\right\},
\qquad m=8\,\mathrm{mm}.
\label{eq:depth-region-of-interest}
\end{equation}
with the same coordinate map as the plane. The distance transform uses
column norms of $Q_i$ as axis spacings. This implements physical
Euclidean distance for orthogonal voxel axes, including rotated grids.
Sheared affines are rejected, as are annotations with fewer than four
foreground points or a projected normal of norm below 0.1. Such
failures require correction of the preparation.

The conventional medical recipes use one $64^3$ volume per update
and apply constraints only to the primary output; nnU-Net's other
supervised resolutions retain their baseline losses. Identity affines
introduced during ADNI export do not establish anatomical orientation
or physical spacing; its distance and depth constructions therefore
use stored array coordinates in voxel units.

\subsection{GeoFWI3D Observation Boundaries}
\label{sec:geo-boundaries}

The final four-model geological campaign retains model-specific band
supports. For axis lengths $N_{i,k}$, define the interior domain
\begin{equation}
R_i^{(r)}=\{v\in\Omega_i:
 r\leq v_k\leq N_{i,k}-1-r\ \text{for }k=1,2,3\}.
\label{eq:geological-band-frame}
\end{equation}
UNETR uses $\widetilde B_i^s=B_i^s\cap R_i^{(2)}$, excluding the
two-voxel observation frame. nnU-Net, SwinUNETR and the compact U-Net
use $\widetilde B_i^s=B_i^s$ without this exclusion. In every recipe,
the band loss is the half-sum of inner and outer BCE sums divided
by their respective counts plus $10^{-6}$. Both sides must be
nonempty; invalid cases are skipped and an entirely invalid batch
returns a differentiable zero.

Edge pairs require both endpoints in
$\widetilde B_i^{\mathrm{in}}\cup\widetilde B_i^{\mathrm{out}}$.
The reduction is an exact pair mean followed by a valid-case mean,
with differentiable zero when no valid case remains. Thus only the
UNETR recipe excludes pairs involving the observation frame.
The single-foreground distance loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SDF},i}^{\mathrm{Geo}}
=\frac{1}{|\Omega_i|}\sum_{v\in\Omega_i}
 \bigl(p_{i,S}(v)-y_{i,S}(v)\bigr)\varphi_{i,S}(v).
\label{eq:geological-sdf-loss}
\end{equation}
It covers the full stored grid, including the observation frame,
and retains the half-voxel shift, unit-grid Euclidean distances and
clipping threshold eight. The coordinate and depth conventions are
specified in Section~\ref{sec:geo-methods}.

\subsection{Sampled Volumetric Supervision in Pretrained Models}
\label{sec:sampled-pretrained-supervision}

Native three-dimensional class masks define separate bands for
$c\in\{0,A,P\}$ on MSD, $c=H$ on ADNI and $c=S$ for SAM3 on
GeoFWI3D. Zero padding is retained, including for MSD background,
whose inner band can therefore reach crop faces. Support counts are
computed over the full volume before sampling.

For $Z_i$ planes, uniform anchor $k$ and plane domain
$\Omega_i^{(k)}$, a class with two nonempty band sides contributes
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{B,i,c}^{(k)}
=-\frac{Z_i}{2}\Biggl[
 &\frac{\sum_{v\in B_{i,c}^{\mathrm{in}}\cap\Omega_i^{(k)}}
                  \log p_{\theta,i,c}(v)}
        {n_{i,c}^{\mathrm{in}}+\varepsilon}\\
 {}+{}&\frac{\sum_{v\in B_{i,c}^{\mathrm{out}}\cap\Omega_i^{(k)}}
                  \log(1-p_{\theta,i,c}(v))}
        {n_{i,c}^{\mathrm{out}}+\varepsilon}\Biggr].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:medsam-sampled-band-loss}
\end{equation}
where $n_{i,c}^s=|B_{i,c}^s|$ and $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$.
For fixed predictions, averaging over anchors recovers the full-volume
stabilized loss. An empty full-volume side gives a zero class contribution;
an empty sampled support also contributes zero without changing the
global denominator.

Let $\mathcal E_{i,c}^B(k)$ contain selected in-plane pairs on $k$
and through-plane pairs from $k$ to $k+1$ for $k<Z_i-1$.
This assigns every full-volume edge to exactly one anchor. With
$n_{i,c}^E=|\mathcal E_{i,c}^B|>0$ and
$\delta_{i,c}=p_{\theta,i,c}-y_{i,c}$,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{E,i,c}^{(k)}
=\frac{Z_i}{n_{i,c}^E}
  \sum_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal{E}_{i,c}^B(k)}
    \bigl(\delta_{i,c}(u)-\delta_{i,c}(v)\bigr)^2.
\label{eq:medsam-sampled-edge-loss}
\end{equation}
satisfies
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{Z_i}\sum_{k=0}^{Z_i-1}
  \widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{E,i,c}^{(k)}
=\frac{1}{n_{i,c}^E}\sum_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal{E}_{i,c}^B}
    \bigl(\delta_{i,c}(u)-\delta_{i,c}(v)\bigr)^2
=\mathcal{L}_{E,i,c}.
\label{eq:medsam-edge-expectation}
\end{equation}
The native supervised objective uses companion plane
$(k+1)\bmod Z_i$, but the edge estimator never joins the final
plane to the first. Empty full-volume edge support contributes zero.

On MSD, both estimators average all three classes with weight $1/3$,
including zero-contributing classes. ADNI MedSAM3 and GeoFWI3D SAM3
evaluate their single foreground mask, with no additional
background-class average. Volumes and anchors are sampled
uniformly, and the two pairs per update are averaged through the
fixed accumulation schedule. For fixed slice predictions, the finite
expectation identities also apply to their gradients. They provide
volumetric supervision while retaining an image-based encoder.


Signed-distance and depth supervision also admit sampled volumetric
reductions. Let $C_+$ be the foreground class set, let each plane
contain $N_i$ voxels and let $w_i(v)$ be full-volume normalized depth
weights. Their anchor estimators are
\begin{align}
\widehat{\mathcal L}_{S,i}^{(k)}
 &=\frac{1}{|C_+|N_i}\sum_{c\in C_+}
   \sum_{v\in\Omega_i^{(k)}}(p_{i,c}(v)-y_{i,c}(v))\varphi_{i,c}(v),
   \label{eq:medsam-sampled-sdf}\\
\widehat{\mathcal L}_{D,i}^{(k)}
 &=-Z_i\sum_{v\in\Omega_i^{(k)}}w_i(v)\log p_{i,y_i(v)}(v).
   \label{eq:medsam-sampled-depth}
\end{align}
Uniform anchor averaging recovers the full-volume SDF mean and weighted
cross-entropy. The $Z_i$ correction is needed for the full-volume depth
weights, whereas SDF already averages within each plane. Distances use the
verified 1 mm MSD grid and unit source-array coordinates for ADNI and
GeoFWI3D SAM3, as specified in the methods. The latter two constructions
do not infer physical millimetres or anatomical orientation from their
working coordinate maps.

\subsection{Calibration and Discrete Compute Limits}

Sequential calibration adds weighted gradient vectors before measuring
their RMS. For example,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{RMS}(g_{i,0}+\lambda_B g_{i,B})^2
={}&\operatorname{RMS}(g_{i,0})^2
 +2\lambda_B\langle g_{i,0},g_{i,B}\rangle_{m_i}\\
 &+\lambda_B^2\operatorname{RMS}(g_{i,B})^2,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:calibration-cross-term}
\end{equation}
where $\langle a,b\rangle_{m_i}=m_i^{-1}\sum_k a_kb_k$.
The cross term retains directional agreement. Adding norms instead
would generally give another coefficient. Quantiles use linear
interpolation, and calibration requires finite positive ratios,
without an arbitrary fallback for an invalid result.

For early activation $d_a$, delayed SDF activation $d_a^S$ and
profiled per-update costs $f_{a,0},f_{a,1},f_{a,2}$ in the three
phases, the charge is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_a(n)={}&f_{a,0}\min(n,d_a)\\
 &+f_{a,1}\bigl[\min(n,d_a^S)-d_a\bigr]_+\\
 &+f_{a,2}[n-d_a^S]_+,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:piecewise-optimization-cost}
\end{equation}
with $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$. The largest integer update count satisfying
the ceiling is permitted. The delayed boundary is chosen nearest
to charge $5C_{\max}/9$, after the early ramp, with ties resolved
toward the earlier boundary. Shared activation requires only two
cost phases.

Attempted updates are charged even when a numerical failure prevents
a parameter change; successful updates are tracked separately.
Fractional final updates are not permitted. Calibration remains
outside this optimization ledger.

\section{Reproducibility Material}
\label{sec:evidence-register}
\label{sec:background-dice-evidence}
\label{sec:all-variant-error-evidence}

The project archive links run identifiers to source versions,
configurations, fold and seed inventories, support/evaluation manifests,
calibrated coefficients and final metrics. The directories below, relative
to \path{reports/}, contain the thesis extracts and their verification
records. Their scripts and metadata identify the underlying campaign
archives; full training-set scores and update counts remain available
in the run-level CSV/JSON files.

\begin{center}
\small\setstretch{1.05}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{@{}P{3.8cm}P{11.2cm}@{}}
\hline
Evidence & Archive entry point \\
\hline
Conventional GeoFWI3D & \path{thesis_geofwi_update_20261022/} \\
MSD/ADNI conventional and MedSAM3 & \path{thesis_noaug_update_20261021/} \\
Complete SAM3 results & \path{thesis_sam3_final_20261023/} \\
Per-volume confusion matrices and Dice & \path{background_dice_archive_recovery_20261023/} \\
Complementary Dice tables & \path{thesis_background_dice_update_20261023/} \\
Full-validation error tables & \path{thesis_all_variant_errors_20261023/} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Aggregates are reconstructed from final run records, averaging seeds
within each fold and then weighting folds equally. Reported dispersion
is the sample standard deviation of fold means. Paired gains retain the
baseline for the same fold, seed and execution batch, including the
distinct matched controls for MSD MedSAM3 $B$ and $B+E$. Verification
records distinguish inventory checks, numerical recalculation and
available payload-hash checks; these checks use retained records rather
than rerunning training or inference.

Saved confusion matrices support recalculation of per-volume Dice and
full-grid errors. The complete SAM3 extraction supplies the final 126
evaluations and replaces the earlier SAM3 snapshots in the complementary
Dice and error extracts. For MSD MedSAM3 $B$, $B+E$ and their control,
the 27 aggregate foreground scores and error records are preserved;
their per-volume matrices were not recovered. Consequently, their
background and all-class scores remain unavailable, as stated in
Section~\ref{sec:background-dice-results}. All six studies nevertheless
have completed results for their declared objective inventories.

Detailed archive inventories, execution histories and verification
receipts remain with the corresponding research records.
