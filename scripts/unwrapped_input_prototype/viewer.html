What this prototype establishes

The transformation uses the predicted foreground union, never the annotation or its A/P boundary. The full MRI and surrounding context are resampled into a 64 × 64 × 64 volume. Signed depth stays unchanged, and each transformed coordinate has an analytic inverse.

This is geometric straightening, not a cortical or laminar unfolding. It preserves volume but changes local angles and distances. MRI interpolation can blur detail even when coordinate inversion is exact. The geometry here comes from the full-data model; a 5% experiment would need a predictor trained only on that subset.
