**One-minute explanation of the frozen recipe:**

“The frozen formulation keeps three-millimetre inside and two-millimetre outside bands, emphasizing the hardest 70% inside and 10% outside. It combines whole-side and selected errors with 65% selected emphasis inside and 85% outside, and keeps independent targets at one- and three-millimetre endpoint separations. We then rebalance the band and endpoint strengths and add depth-balanced three-class cross-entropy. The selected development run reaches 81.502%, 0.751 points below the strongest recorded native branch. More importantly, nine fresh runs across three folds and three seeds average 80.217% with a 1.069-point run SD. This establishes repeatability of scores; paired gains under the separate FLOP cap remain architecture-dependent.”

The two later selected milestones are **81.324%** from band/endpoint gradient targets of 12.5%/7.5%, and **81.502%** after adding depth CE at a 10% gradient target relative to the complete outer objective. These gradient targets are calibrated on training cases, not fixed percentages of loss values. Depth balances signed 2 mm anatomical bins within annotated foreground plus 8 mm, with reduced mass for bins below 64 voxels. This geometry is training supervision; inference remains MRI-only.

**Terminology:** band width is distance from the voxel centre to the reference boundary; endpoint distance is total inside-to-outside separation. A selection fraction says how many voxels are selected, emphasis says how their mean is mixed with the whole-side mean, and side weight says how inside and outside contribute to the total. These are different controls. The selected voxels are recomputed from the current predictions at each forward pass; the loss coefficients are calibrated on training data and then fixed.

## Two-sentence descriptions of all plotted variants

| Point | A/P Dice | Configuration | Explanation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | 74.304% | Dice only | Train the ordinary three-class model with the baseline soft Dice loss. This is the matched control for the current progression and adds no boundary-specific objective. |
| 2 | 76.727% | Original bands | Add binary cross-entropy on annotated inner and outer foreground bands. Average the two sides separately and balance them equally so that one side's voxel count does not dominate. |
| 3 | 77.303% | Original bands + edge | Add the original squared error in signed foreground-probability differences between adjacent voxels. This supervises local transitions and same-side consistency within the original band support. |
| 4 | 77.317% | Equal same-side and crossing-edge weights | Split the original edge pairs into those with equal reference labels and those crossing the foreground boundary. Average those two groups separately and give them equal weight so the more numerous group does not dominate. |
| 5 | 77.393% | One-millimetre comparisons balanced per anchor | Compare foreground probabilities across one-millimetre annotated crossings, encouraging the inside endpoint toward higher probability than the outside endpoint. Give each eligible inside boundary anchor equal total weight, splitting its contribution over available outward directions. |
| 6 | 77.647% | One- and three-millimetre probability comparisons | Supervise the probability contrast across both short and wider annotated crossings. Average pairs within each distance and give the two distances equal weight, providing feedback at two spatial scales. |
| 7 | 77.855% | Three-millimetre comparisons on common support | Use the wider probability comparisons on annotated faces that satisfy the common eligibility condition for three-millimetre crossings. Restricting eligibility makes the related distance comparisons less sensitive to changes in which boundary sites are sampled. |
| 8 | 78.132% | Independent one- and three-millimetre targets | Replace the coupled contrast error with separate squared membership errors for inside and outside endpoints at the same two distances. Each complete objective is calibrated according to its protocol, so the chart compares calibrated training configurations rather than a single coefficient held numerically constant throughout.

## How to describe the branch reference

“The native auxiliary branch separately encodes MRI from a guide-derived hippocampal region and fuses those features with the native segmentation network. Its best recorded fold-0/seed-0 variant uses Dice, original bands and original edge supervision, reaching 82.253% A/P Dice.”

The native branch with Dice alone reaches 81.974%, so the architecture contributes substantially before adding these losses. The dashed line is not the unfolded branch, and its computational cost has not been matched to the plain-network search in this figure.

## What the progress means

The observed gap from original bands to the branch is 5.526 pp. The historical 81.112% milestone reduced it to 1.141 pp; the subsequently frozen 81.502% recipe reduces it to **0.751 pp**, closing about 86% of the observed development-score gap. This is not a matched replicated branch comparison, an equal-compute result or a causal estimate.

Use “approaching the branch's development validation Dice, with repeatable performance across the tested seeds and folds.” Avoid “equivalent to the branch,” “same mechanism,” “better on every anatomical metric,” or “confirmed generalization.” The final recipe reduces extra foreground but increases missed tissue relative to original bands in the development cell. Seed 2 has lower Dice and worse HD95 in each replicated fold; the detailed tables are in the briefing.
