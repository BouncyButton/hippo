# Multi-view hippocampus relationship study — 22 September 2026

This study tested whether sagittal and axial information, separately or together with coronal information, identifies a safer A/P cut or a useful whole-hippocampus boundary signal. All evaluations use the two previously studied MSD Swin validation folds (52 patients each), 64³ preprocessed images, and the early-stopped Swin probabilities in `cst_early_stopped_reanalysis_665422`. The folds are patient-disjoint for the cross-fold risk-head test. They are **not** an untouched prospective test set.

## Main findings

1. **There is substantial oracle room at the A/P interface.** Among 260 labels, 202 are exactly separable by a single coronal cut; 242 have at most 1% class voxels on the wrong side of their best cut. If the true cut is supplied, relabeling Swin's *existing* foreground improves mean Dice by +0.0186/+0.0245 in folds 0/1 and fixes most class-swap voxels. This is a label-informed upper bound, not a deployable result. Swin's own cut is already within 0.885/1.058 voxels MAE of the true cut.
2. **MRI-only cut regression did not locate patient-specific cuts.** Matched coronal, sagittal, axial, 32-token tri-view, and 96-token tri-view CST regressors have held-out cut MAE around 1.24–1.25 voxels in fold 0 and 1.36–1.38 in fold 1, all worse than Swin's cut. Their output varies far less than the true patient cut.
3. **Mask-conditioned candidate ranking collapsed to a cut prior.** On held-out Swin masks, the tri-view 32/96-token rankers choose a single coordinate per fold despite true-cut standard deviations of about 1.6 voxels. Their cut MAE is 1.212/1.385 versus Swin's 0.885/1.058; relabeling changes mean Dice by −0.0083/−0.0072. Three-single-view and all-view rank ensembles also lose to Swin. Giving the tri-view rankers the *ground-truth foreground union* does not change their selected cuts. In fold 0, separately zeroing either the MRI or mask input channels does not change either tri-view ranker's choices. Thus the current objective/architecture does not extract a useful patient-specific interface cue; predicted-mask domain shift alone does not explain the failure.
4. **Multi-view MRI-to-profile teachers are not reliable boundary QC.** Their tri-view CST–Swin disagreement at the patient level captures only 21–22% of all foreground-boundary error voxels in the highest-scored 20% of patients, close to random selection. Single-view and component correlations change substantially between folds. Several teacher profile errors are worse than the optimistic *held-out mean-profile* diagnostic baseline; low absolute profile MAE alone was misleading. Boundary errors total 36,059/37,065 voxels versus 3,512/4,512 A/P swaps in folds 0/1.
5. **Explicit 3D cut consensus is nearly neutral and not safe.** Soft class-probability voting, confident voting, and robust averages of sagittal/axial interface traces only shift the cut by a few cases. The best mean Dice deltas are +0.00037/+0.00099 in folds 0/1, but still harm 17/14 of 52 patients, respectively.
6. **A modest whole-mask shape-QC clue survives both folds.** An unsupervised five-component PCA prior fitted to *training-label* A/P class area profiles in each view, then averaged across standardized sagittal/coronal/axial anomaly scores on held-out Swin masks, correlates with boundary-error count at r=0.291/0.335 and boundary-error *rate* at r=0.267/0.391. The riskiest 20% capture 23.2%/25.6% of boundary-error voxels. Predicted foreground volume alone captures 21.5%/23.7% and has boundary-rate r=−0.266/0.042, so the shape signal is not simply volume. However, its selection lift is small; it is not a correction rule. A fixed ridge head trained on fold 0 and evaluated on fold 1 (and vice versa) does **not** improve boundary-risk capture over the unsupervised score. Swap-risk transfer is asymmetric, so it is not a reliable A/P detector.

## Interpretation

The useful surviving representation is an **anomaly score for the whole predicted shape**, not a learned anatomical cut location. The mask-shape PCA result is a transparent negative control to the CST proposals, not evidence that the CST learned cross-view correspondence. It flags somewhat difficult cases, especially when normalizing boundary error by true foreground size, but cannot say what local edit to make. The particularly large oracle cut gain demonstrates the value of the target only if a genuinely patient-specific cut estimator becomes available. The tri-view CSTs share set attention and view/position metadata but do **not** explicitly align the same 3D coordinates across intersecting planes; their negative result does not rule out an intersection-aware architecture.

This finite hypothesis round is exhausted: direct MRI-only regression, MRI+candidate-mask ranking, matched-view score ensembling, MRI-to-shape teacher disagreement, and explicit 3D interface consensus did not yield a safe correction. The next distinct experiment, if pursued, should train a **mask-conditioned, cross-view boundary-anomaly detector** with patient-held-out synthetic protrusions, holes, truncations, and A/P interface shifts, compare against the simple PCA score and Swin uncertainty, and require cross-fold calibration plus a no-harm abstention rule before any segmentation edit. Do not apply the current cut ranker or shape prior as a hard constraint.
