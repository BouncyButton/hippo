Semantic segmentation assigns a class to each location in an image, allowing structures such as organs to be identified and measured. In medical imaging, segmentation supports the interpretation of scans and can contribute to diagnosis and prognosis. Developing an accurate segmentation model, however, requires time, annotated data and computational resources. This thesis investigates whether spatial information already contained in annotations can improve three-dimensional segmentation when labelled data and training computation are limited.

The approach uses Logic Tensor Networks (LTNs), which combine neural learning with knowledge expressed as logical statements, and their logarithmic formulation, logLTN. Four additional training objectives encourage correct labels near boundaries and correct label transitions between neighbouring locations, weight errors by distance from class boundaries, and balance supervision across layers of a volume. These objectives reuse existing annotations without requiring additional manual labelling.

The medical experiments concern hippocampus segmentation, relevant to the study of memory and Alzheimer's disease, using the Medical Segmentation Decathlon (MSD) and Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) datasets. Salt-body segmentation in synthetic GeoFWI3D volumes tests whether the benefits extend beyond medical imaging. Experiments cover four architectures trained from scratch under matched computational budgets within each dataset, and adaptation of pretrained Segment Anything Model 3 (SAM3) and its medical specialization, MedSAM3, using small labelled support sets.

For every architecture and dataset studied from scratch, at least one tested combination improves mean validation Dice, a measure of segmentation overlap, over the matched baseline. The largest gains are 11.97 percentage points in the medical experiments and 6.14 points in the geological experiments. The best tested adaptation configurations improve Dice by 3.45 to 5.38 points. The preferred combination varies across models and tasks, and adding more constraints does not consistently improve performance. These findings support annotation-derived spatial supervision as a useful training strategy under limited resources, with benefits demonstrated in both medical and geological segmentation.
